# Questions and raw answers

## 1. What made this project more interesting than a standard airline redesign?

SunExpress had been a long-term client, with the agency previously producing smaller website updates and campaign work.

The redesign represented the first opportunity to rethink the entire experience rather than individual pages.

Alexander describes this as one of his oldest projects but still one of the pieces he is most proud of because much of the underlying structure survived for roughly a decade, which is unusually long for digital products.

The challenge was not making the airline appear premium.

Instead, the ambition was to make an inexpensive airline feel friendly, optimistic and enjoyable while remaining clearly price-led.

The website should encourage travellers to discover destinations, packages and offers without overwhelming them.

---

### Follow-up

What did "discovery" actually mean?

Discovery meant allowing travellers to browse destinations and offers naturally instead of treating the booking flow as a purely transactional process.

Price remained the primary decision-making tool, but destinations, travel inspiration and package options were deliberately given a stronger role.

---

## 2. Which design mechanism best represents your contribution?

Alexander repeatedly argued against fragmenting the booking experience into many disconnected screens.

Instead of forcing travellers through separate pages for flight selection, package selection and extras, related decisions were brought together where possible.

One example was allowing travellers to select their flight and associated package within the same view.

This helped users understand how pricing worked instead of treating each decision as an isolated step.

Another contribution was creating a visual seat-selection experience, which Alexander remembers as relatively uncommon at the time.

Throughout the booking journey, simplicity consistently took priority over excessive customisation.

The tone of voice was intentionally friendlier than typical airline booking systems, avoiding unnecessarily formal or intimidating language while still communicating operational details.

---

### Follow-up

Why did that matter?

Keeping related decisions together reduced unnecessary navigation and built a clearer mental model of how the booking worked.

Instead of repeatedly asking users to context-switch, the interface progressively introduced complexity only when needed.

---

## 3. Looking back, what are you still proud of?

Beyond the booking flow itself, Alexander remains proud that the interface retained warmth and personality during a period when flat design dominated digital products.

Rather than following minimalist trends completely, the team tried to preserve visual richness without introducing clutter.

Important areas of work included:

- developing a coherent colour system and rationale;
- designing a custom icon language;
- creating a complete illustration system;
- producing a reusable digital style guide that could extend beyond the core booking experience into marketing pages and destination content.

Alexander describes himself as the principal designer on the project.

His contributions included:

- UX;
- interface design;
- interaction design;
- prototyping;
- motion;
- photography selection;
- helping establish the overall visual language.

Destination imagery intentionally focused on presenting places from their most optimistic and inviting perspective.

# Most valuable material

## Fascinating observation

This project is noteworthy because its structure remained relevant for around a decade.

Although parts of the visual design have inevitably aged, the information architecture and booking decisions proved unusually durable.

## Design mechanism

Progressive simplification.

Instead of exposing every booking branch immediately, the interface grouped related decisions together, reduced unnecessary screens and revealed complexity gradually.

At the same time, the visual language remained expressive enough to encourage exploration rather than reducing everything to functional utility.

## Strongest anecdote

The decision to merge flight selection and package selection into one coherent interaction, combined with an early visual seat-selection experience, illustrates Alexander's approach to reducing cognitive effort while improving understanding.
