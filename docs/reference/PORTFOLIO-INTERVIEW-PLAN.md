# Portfolio Interview Plan

## Factual source of truth

This document defines the interview method. Project facts, source provenance, open questions, readiness, and the American Heart Association hold state live in `docs/portfolio/`. Do not promote wording from this plan into final copy unless the relevant project claim is confirmed there.

## What to expect

We will work through one project at a time. The conversation should feel like remembering the work with a thoughtful colleague, not completing a questionnaire.

I will ask two or three questions at a time, listen for the parts with energy or tension, and follow those before moving on. Short, imperfect answers are useful. The final prose comes later.

## Before each interview

Bring whatever helps memory return:

- an old portfolio or case study;
- project decks, screenshots, videos, notes, or prototypes;
- names and roles of close collaborators;
- anything that records what was designed, tested, approved, shipped, or dropped.

Old material is evidence and memory support, not copy to reuse automatically.

## The conversation

### 1. Establish the facts

- What was the project, and why was it happening?
- When was it, how long did it run, and what stage was the work in?
- What was your actual role, regardless of the title on paper?
- Who were the important collaborators?

### 2. Find the story

- What was difficult, strange, political, or unusually important?
- What did the original brief miss?
- Which moment or decision changed the direction?
- What did you personally fight for, compromise on, or let go?

### 3. Make the contribution visible

- What did you make with your own hands?
- What did you direct, facilitate, or help another person do well?
- Where did somebody else's expertise improve the work?
- How did you explain or defend the direction to sceptical people?

### 4. Match evidence to the story

- Which image best shows the problem?
- Which artefact shows the decision rather than only the final polish?
- Is there a rejected direction, working file, prototype, or implementation state worth showing?
- What ten or more images and videos would create a complete visual rhythm?

### 5. End honestly

- What changed that we can observe without inventing a statistic?
- What was designed but did not ship?
- What remains unknown?
- What would you do differently now?
- What did the project change in how you work?

## What I will produce after each interview

1. A compact fact sheet with confirmed and unconfirmed details separated.
2. A recommended narrative angle unique to that project.
3. A first human-written draft of roughly 450–700 words.
4. A media sequence with a role for every image or video.
5. A short gap list for missing evidence, credits, dates, or approvals.
6. A version ready to review in the live portfolio.

Nothing marked unconfirmed becomes a public claim. We will use `TBC`, `draft`, or a direct question until the user confirms it.
