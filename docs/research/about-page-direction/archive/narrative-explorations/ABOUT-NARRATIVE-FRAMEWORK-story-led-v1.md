# About Me narrative framework

## Purpose

This document is the evergreen structure for the About Me story. It is not a finished script and it is not tied to one animation treatment. Its job is to keep future writing, proof and interaction serving one coherent dramatic movement.

The three earlier voice routes are archived in `archive/narrative-explorations/ABOUT-STORY-STRUCTURE-ROUTES-v1.md`. They remain examples of how the framework might be populated, but they do not define it.

## The narrative engine

### Central tension

**Clarity can remove the character that made an experience worth engaging with.**

That is the page's organising tension. Complexity contains detail, context and possibility. Design gives it form, but reduction can also make the result anonymous. Alexander's story demonstrates a practice built around recognising what matters, creating structure and protecting specificity.

The public copy does not need to use the phrase “without losing the soul.” Within the framework, **soul** means the specific character, response, surprise and point of view that should survive the process of creating clarity.

### Dramatic question

**Can something become clear without becoming less itself?**

The page should raise this question, test it through Alexander's trajectory and practice, then answer it through the work rather than through a slogan.

### Working story spine: What must survive

This is a story about learning to give complex things form without removing their character.

It begins before the biography, with a difficult choice: clarity always asks us to decide what matters. Alexander's personal lens then shows where that sensitivity came from—first by learning to notice composition, form and shape, then by carrying the same attention into interactions, journeys and systems. As the scale of the work grows, clarity becomes more useful but the risk of generic order grows with it. The present practice answers that tension by bringing structure and character into the same process. The ending does not claim the problem is solved forever; it opens the method toward a new collaboration.

### Transformation

**complexity → attention → structure → character → possibility**

| State | Narrative meaning | Reader experience | Background counterpoint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Complexity | There is more here than can be understood at once. | Curiosity and anticipation | A dense spatial field is visible ahead; the reader approaches rather than being overwhelmed immediately. |
| Attention | Looking carefully reveals relationships and choices. | Recognition and trust | Local patterns become perceptible; the environment recedes while prose asks for concentration. |
| Structure | Form makes complexity usable at a larger scale. | Momentum and confidence | Depth opens or resolves into an organised field. |
| Character | Order becomes meaningful when judgement, response and surprise remain present. | Warmth and distinctiveness | The ordered field gains restrained irregularity, pulse or wave without losing its system. |
| Possibility | The method is not a closed doctrine; it creates room for what comes next. | Release and invitation | The space opens and settles, remaining subtly alive. |

This is not a story in which chaos is bad and a grid is good. It is a story about learning to recognise what matters, giving it structure, and preserving the qualities that make the result feel specific.

## The five-act architecture

### Act 1 — Threshold: promise a story

**Narrative state:** Complexity  
**Page mode:** Spatial  
**Reader question:** What is this really about, and why should I continue?

#### Dramatic job

Open a tension that the rest of the page can resolve. This is not required to be a personal hook. Its more important job is to make a promise: the reader is entering a story with an idea at its centre.

The opening should be broad enough to matter beyond Alexander, but precise enough to feel observable. It should create an unfinished thought rather than deliver the conclusion.

#### Required content

- One recognisable tension between clarity and loss.
- A reason to continue into the page.
- No biography, client list or capability inventory.

#### Copy contract

- 8–22 words across one to three spatial statements.
- Centred, sentence case or restrained display case.
- One idea per reading plane.
- It must work when read without animation.
- It may use “I” only if a genuinely distinctive personal observation is stronger than the central premise.

#### Three valid opening modes

| Mode | What it does | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tension statement — recommended** | Names the difficult judgement at the heart of the work. | Can sound like a slogan if the next act does not make it personal. |
| **Story promise** | Signals the transformation the reader is about to witness. | Can feel self-conscious if it literally announces a story. |
| **Provocative question** | Opens a gap the rest of the page will answer. | Can feel like marketing copy if it is too polished or rhetorical. |

#### Background role

Create distance, depth and anticipation. The reader should sense something complex ahead and choose to enter it. The visual should not yet explain the thesis or resolve into a neat symbol.

#### Exit condition

By the end of this act, the reader should understand that the page is about a judgement deeper than “making things simple,” while still wanting to know why Alexander is the person telling it.

#### Failure modes

- Beginning with a universal claim such as “the world is complex.”
- Beginning with school, posters or a résumé fact.
- Announcing the entire conclusion before any evidence has been earned.
- Using abstract noun clusters such as context, contradiction, humanity and nuance.
- Describing what the animation is doing.

### Act 2 — Personal lens: show how the point of view formed

**Narrative state:** Attention  
**Page mode:** Editorial  
**Reader question:** Why is Alexander the person telling this story?

#### Dramatic job

Move from a shared tension to a specific way of seeing. The biography enters here because it now answers a question created by the opening.

The foundation is not “I used to make posters.” It is learning to notice how composition, form, shape, type, image, space and sequence alter what something communicates and how it feels.

#### Required content

- The visual foundation: composition, form and shape, supported selectively by type, image, space or sequence.
- A transferable lesson about attention and choice.
- A glimpse of personality or curiosity through one concrete detail.
- Communication Design in Mainz only as a cliff note if it grounds the claim.

#### Copy contract

- 45–75 words.
- Large sentence-case prose, left aligned at a comfortable reading width.
- One or two paragraphs, revealed by line or complete sentence.
- Prefer a specific observation, exercise or habit over a complete chronology.

#### Background role

Recede enough to support sustained reading. Small local alignments may suggest attention finding pattern, but movement should remain peripheral and quiet.

#### Optional proof

A small mixed set of studies, fragments or experiments may sit beside the prose. A poster can be one item in that set, never the emblem of the whole foundation. Every artefact needs a short reason for being there: what it trained, tested or revealed.

#### Exit condition

The reader should now understand where Alexander's attention to detail came from and see that the opening premise has a lived basis.

#### Failure modes

- Turning university into the origin myth.
- Listing media without naming the lesson they produced.
- Letting early work occupy more space than present practice.
- Romanticising craft without connecting it to decisions.

### Act 3 — Expansion: increase the scale and stakes

**Narrative state:** Structure emerging  
**Page mode:** Spatial, with an optional short editorial bridge  
**Reader question:** How did that visual foundation become the work he does now?

#### Dramatic job

Change the scale of the story. Individual visual decisions become interactions; interactions become journeys; journeys connect into products, services, brands and systems. The same attention now has consequences for users, teams and organisations.

This is the hinge, not a second biography section. It should feel like acceleration.

#### Required content

- One clear expansion from surface to behaviour to system.
- A sense that responsibility increased with scale.
- No exhaustive chronology of jobs or titles.

#### Copy contract

- 12–32 spatial words.
- Optional 20–40 word editorial bridge only if a factual career transition is essential.
- Two or three centred beats may travel through the reading plane.
- Use cumulative syntax sparingly; every step must add scale or consequence.

#### Background role

Open the depth, alter the topology or let relationships become visible. The environment may begin to organise, but it should not arrive at perfect order yet.

#### Exit condition

The reader should feel that the foundation did not get abandoned; it became useful across more complex and consequential work.

#### Failure modes

- A timeline of employers.
- Literal icons for products, services or systems.
- Treating disciplines as disconnected career changes.
- Repeating “the medium changed” without showing what became larger or harder.

### Act 4 — Practice: show the resolution in action

**Narrative state:** Structure with character  
**Page mode:** Editorial  
**Reader question:** What does Alexander do, how does he work, and what makes the result distinctive?

#### Dramatic job

Pay off the premise with evidence. This is the substantive centre of the page: Alexander makes complex things coherent through a cross-disciplinary practice, then uses judgement, experimentation and craft to keep them from becoming generic.

The act needs an internal turn:

1. **Give complexity structure.** Explain how experience design, systems and prototyping make ideas understandable, testable and usable.
2. **Keep the structure alive.** Explain how art direction, motion, visual delight, creative engineering and AI-assisted image-making contribute character when the problem calls for them.

The argument is not function versus feeling. The distinctive position is refusing the false choice between them.

#### Required content

- Current professional focus and the kinds of problems Alexander takes on.
- A selective set of disciplines explained through what they do.
- International scope and verified client evidence.
- A working principle or method that joins usefulness and character.
- One concrete proof point beyond a list of nouns.

#### Copy contract

- 85–125 words across two or three left-aligned blocks.
- Keep the reading width and type system consistent with Act 2.
- Use outlined boxes only for optional proof, expandable notes or artefacts.
- Client names should sit inside a sentence that explains what their range proves.

#### Background role

Resolve the complex field into an intelligible system, then introduce restrained response: a pulse, wave or material irregularity. The structure remains visible throughout. The copy never narrates this change.

#### Optional proof and interaction

- One prototype demonstrating how an idea became testable.
- One system fragment demonstrating coherence at scale.
- One motion or visual experiment demonstrating character.
- One AI-assisted image with a note about the judgement or refinement Alexander added.
- Selective client references, with agency or team context where required.

Proof may expand, unfold or be dragged briefly, but reading and vertical scroll always remain available.

#### Exit condition

The reader should be able to describe Alexander's practice in one sentence, understand the evidence behind it, and recognise the central standard: the work must function and it must feel considered.

#### Failure modes

- A capability grid with no hierarchy.
- A logo wall presented as a conclusion.
- Claiming innovation without showing a method or result.
- Treating AI as either magic or automation.
- Writing copy that depends on a heartbeat, grid or point cloud to make sense.
- Describing character as decoration applied after the useful work is complete.

### Act 5 — Open ending: turn the story toward the reader

**Narrative state:** Possibility  
**Page mode:** Spatial statement, then editorial links  
**Reader question:** What does Alexander want to make next, and what can I do now?

#### Dramatic job

Convert the answer into momentum. The ending should state one future-facing ambition, then invite the reader into it. It is a release, not a summary of every previous claim.

#### Required content

- One direction Alexander genuinely wants to pursue.
- One primary action: get in touch.
- Secondary exits only after the main invitation.

#### Copy contract

- 18–38 words plus a short link row.
- One centred closing statement that opens outward.
- The invitation should sound like something Alexander could say to a person.
- Contact is primary; work, LinkedIn and music can remain lighter secondary references.

#### Background role

Open the field and create release. The environment may settle, but it should not become inert; possibility is structured and still alive.

#### Optional proof and levity

Small personal artefacts, music or experiments can appear after the invitation. They should reward curiosity without delaying the call to action or turning the ending into a miscellany.

#### Exit condition

The reader should leave with a clear sense of Alexander's ambition and an easy next step.

#### Failure modes

- Ending on a self-description or polished slogan.
- Introducing a new philosophical argument.
- Offering four actions at the same visual weight.
- Letting playful artefacts become the actual conclusion.

## The dramatic curve

| Act | Story movement | Emotional movement | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Threshold | Open the unresolved tension. | Curiosity | A difficult question appears. |
| 2. Personal lens | Earn the right to explore it. | Recognition | The question gains a personal source. |
| 3. Expansion | Raise the scale and consequences. | Momentum | Visual judgement becomes experience and system work. |
| 4. Practice | Resolve the tension through evidence. | Trust, then character | Structure and expression become one practice. |
| 5. Open ending | Hand the possibility to the reader. | Release | The answer becomes an invitation. |

The narrative climax belongs inside Act 4, not in the animation alone. Its function is to join usefulness and character in plain language and demonstrate that they are part of the same practice.

## Page rhythm and information budget

### Alternating rhythm

**Spatial threshold → editorial lens → spatial expansion → editorial practice → spatial invitation**

The three spatial acts carry compression, scale and emotional tempo. The two editorial acts carry biography, evidence and nuance. The pattern prevents both the text and the point field from becoming continuous spectacle.

### Working word budget

| Act | Core words | Reading role |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| 1. Threshold | 8–22 | Slow spatial emphasis |
| 2. Personal lens | 45–75 | Calm prose |
| 3. Expansion | 12–32 | Fast spatial hinge |
| 4. Practice | 85–125 | Main informational passage |
| 5. Open ending | 18–38 | Slow close and CTA |
| **Total** | **168–292** | Target the lower-middle of the range after facts are verified. |

A first complete draft may reach 240–275 words. The publishing target should be approximately **200–240 core words**, excluding optional proof labels and link text. That leaves space for animation holds while keeping the main story readable in about one minute.

## Information allocation

| Information | Primary home | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Central belief | Acts 1 and 4 | Raise it first; prove and resolve it later. |
| Visual foundation | Act 2 | Explain what it trained, not everything that was made. |
| University | Act 2 footnote | Include only if it adds factual grounding. |
| Posters and early artefacts | Act 2 optional proof or Act 5 levity | One example among many, never the opening premise. |
| Professional trajectory | Act 3 | Express as an expansion of scale, not a complete timeline. |
| Current disciplines | Act 4 | Explain through effects and decisions. |
| Clients | Act 4 proof | Use selectively to demonstrate range, trust or context. |
| AI image generation | Act 4 proof | Pair the capability with an example and Alexander's judgement. |
| Personality and side projects | Acts 2 or 5 | Use small, specific details; avoid a separate hobbies biography. |
| Aspiration | Act 5 | State one credible direction, not a grand prediction. |
| Contact and links | Act 5 | One primary invitation, then secondary exits. |

## Relationship between text and background

The story must make sense as a plain text document. The background contributes emotion, pacing and spatial memory; it does not provide missing logic.

Use this division of labour:

- **Text says what the page means.**
- **Proof shows why the reader should believe it.**
- **Background makes the transformation felt.**
- **Interaction rewards attention without interrupting the story.**

The point system may move through complexity, local alignment, structure, pulse and openness. Copy should not mention clouds, grids, waves, travelling, heartbeat or flattening merely to justify those effects.

## Proof and artefact rules

1. Every artefact must support a nearby claim or reveal a real aspect of personality.
2. A proof item receives a reason, not just a label: what it changed, tested or taught.
3. Optional interactions may reveal depth, but required facts remain visible in the core story.
4. One strong example is better than a carousel of uncontextualised work.
5. Early work stays subordinate to the present practice.
6. Client evidence acknowledges collaborators or agency context where needed.
7. Playfulness arrives as a reward, not as visual noise behind substantial prose.
8. Reduced-motion and touch users receive the same narrative, evidence and hierarchy.

## Framework acceptance test

Before a route moves into storyboarding, confirm all of the following:

- The first act opens a tension or promise; it does not begin the biography.
- The reader can state the central dramatic question after Act 1.
- “I” enters because it adds origin, action or accountability.
- The trajectory expands the scale of one practice rather than presenting disconnected disciplines.
- The central claim is resolved with methods and evidence in Act 4.
- Structure and character are treated as interdependent, not sequential polish.
- The full story works without seeing the background.
- Each animation state has a distinct emotional job.
- Every optional artefact supports a claim or a personal detail.
- The main story remains readable in approximately one minute.
- The ending names one ambition and one primary next step.
- The result sounds direct, observant and human when read aloud.
