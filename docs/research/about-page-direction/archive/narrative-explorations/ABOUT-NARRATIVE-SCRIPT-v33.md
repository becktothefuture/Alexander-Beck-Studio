# About Me narrative script v33

I’m Alex—a designer and creative technologist working across products, systems and interactive experiences.

I’ve always been drawn to complex problems.

Especially the ones that cross disciplines.

I studied Communication Design because I liked approaching the same idea through type, image and motion. It taught me not to choose the medium before understanding the problem.

I’ve worked in agencies, startups and independently. At Denaline, I collaborated with developers on interfaces, icons and motion. That got me thinking about what screens did, not only how they looked. At Yoti, identity verification made product, brand and trust part of the same problem.

I also speak several languages. The same thought can sound direct in one and awkward in another, which is probably why context matters so much to me. I now look at product, brand and implementation together from the start.

I start by talking to the people closest to the problem and checking the brief’s assumptions. Then I make something—a sketch, prototype, motion study or code—for everyone to react to.

I often help set the direction, but I do it in the open: sharing work early, explaining my choices and leaving room for other people to improve it.

On a financial index-building project, complexity had to be layered rather than hidden. Some interaction nuance was lost in the build, changing how closely I stay involved through delivery.

[Project impressions]

Unresolved questions often become side projects. Right now, I’m rethinking the iOS keyboard because I use it every day and think it could be better. I keep returning to similar questions about AI, robotics, trust and privacy.

Complex problems are still the interesting ones.

Especially when different disciplines have to meet.

That is usually where something new starts.

Let’s talk.

If you’re working through one, I’d like to hear about it.
