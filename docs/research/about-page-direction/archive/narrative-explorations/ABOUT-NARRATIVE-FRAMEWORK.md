# About Me narrative framework

## Status

**Active narrative direction.** Rewritten after reviewing the current lab in motion and reconnecting it to the questionnaire material, source matrix and creative-direction research.

This framework replaces the profile-led eight-beat structure. The About page should feel like entering an argument and travelling through a way of thinking. Professional facts still matter, but they support the story rather than becoming the story.

## What the current experience gets wrong

The current lab has a strong spatial rhythm but a weak narrative hierarchy.

- Repeated labels such as **Profile**, **Trajectory** and **Practice** tell the reader how to file information instead of letting meaning accumulate.
- The role, employer, client ledger and future-interest list arrive as profile data. Together they make the middle read like LinkedIn in cinematic clothing.
- Large spatial sentences behave like chapter headings between résumé sections rather than discoveries produced by the journey.
- The background changes are atmospheric but replaceable. They do not yet carry the argument.
- The page explains breadth repeatedly, but rarely demonstrates the judgement that connects it.

The correction is not to make every sentence poetic. It is to restore one clear dramatic idea, let the point cloud embody its changing states, and use factual prose only where evidence is needed.

## Audience promise

The page serves three depths of attention without becoming three different pages.

1. A fast reader should leave with one memorable point of view about complexity and clarity.
2. A creative leader, collaborator or client should understand how Alexander approaches an unresolved problem.
3. A reader who wants evidence should find selective career, project and client context in quieter detail text.

No reader needs a complete chronology. LinkedIn remains the exhaustive professional record.

## Narrative engine

### Central tension

Every act of clarity is also an act of editing. The difficult question is not simply what can be removed, but what must survive.

### Dramatic question

**Can complexity be given form without losing the character, context and possibility that made it meaningful?**

### Working thesis

**Good design finds the structure inside complexity without flattening what gives it life.**

This is not finished public copy. It is the argument that every sentence, proof point and spatial transformation must serve.

### Story movement

**density → attention → structure → stillness → response → possibility**

This is not a story in which chaos is bad and order is good. Order is a necessary but incomplete answer. The conceptual turn happens when a perfectly legible system becomes too still, then gains response, variation and life without losing its structure.

## The point cloud is a co-narrator

The final 3D point cloud is not a background image or a visual reward between paragraphs. It is the material through which the argument becomes perceptible.

| Point-cloud state | What the reader experiences | Narrative meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Density** | A deep field contains more than can be read at once. | Complexity is real material, not noise waiting to be deleted. |
| **Signal** | Local relationships hold, brighten or briefly align. | Clarity begins with attention. |
| **Structure** | The field resolves into an intelligible plane or system. | Form makes complexity navigable and usable. |
| **Stillness** | The system becomes precise, stable and intentionally lifeless. | Order alone is not enough. |
| **Response** | One restrained pulse spreads into variation, depth and movement. | Character lives inside behaviour, judgement and response. |
| **Possibility** | The field opens while remaining subtly alive. | A good system creates room for what comes next. |

The material remains continuous across the page. Vertical prose sections quieten or reframe the field; they do not cut to unrelated decorative scenes. Spatial sentences occupy stable reading planes inside the same volume and arrive from the centre of the viewport.

The copy must remain understandable with motion removed, but the motion should deepen the reader's understanding rather than merely decorate it.

## Six movements

### 1. Enter the problem

**Mode:** Spatial sentence inside density  
**Reader question:** What is at stake here?

Open with a difficult, observable judgement about clarity and loss. Do not introduce Alexander, a job title or a service proposition. The first sentence should create an unfinished question that the rest of the page earns.

The field should feel compelling rather than hostile. The reader chooses to enter complexity.

### 2. Learn to pay attention

**Mode:** Large vertical prose with one highlighted sentence  
**Reader question:** Why is Alexander the person thinking about this?

Introduce the personal lens through language, technology and visual form. The point is not the education timeline; it is learning that communication depends on emphasis, omission, rhythm, relationships and choice.

Use career or education context only as detail prose. The primary prose should describe the transferable way of seeing.

### 3. Find the structure

**Mode:** Spatial sentence as signal becomes structure  
**Reader question:** What changes when attention becomes useful?

Distinguish complexity from confusion. Let local order become a system the reader can perceive and move through. This is the expansion from composition into interactions, behaviour, trust, products and systems, expressed as increasing consequence rather than a chronology of employers.

### 4. Make the idea tangible

**Mode:** Large vertical prose with selective supporting detail  
**Reader question:** How does this way of thinking operate in real work?

Describe immersion, the organising idea, prototypes, creative direction and collaboration as one connected practice. The main prose should reveal the method. Smaller detail text may provide two or three precise examples, client contexts or artefact descriptions.

Do not use a client ledger as a climax. Proof earns a sentence; it does not become the sentence.

### 5. Refuse the false resolution

**Mode:** Spatial turn from perfect stillness into a pulse  
**Reader question:** Is a clear system enough?

This is the conceptual and emotional hinge. The ordered field should become almost too perfect before one sentence challenges it. Character is not decoration added after utility; it appears through response, movement, tactility, restraint, surprise and visible care.

The point cloud must make this distinction felt: structure remains while behaviour changes.

### 6. Leave the field open

**Mode:** Large prose resolving into one spatial invitation  
**Reader question:** Where can this way of working go next?

Name the consequential unresolved territory Alexander wants to enter without presenting a shopping list of sectors or technologies. The ending should invite a particular kind of problem and collaborator, then release into a simple contact action.

The field remains ordered but not finished.

## Two-size editorial system

The narrative should rely on two content sizes, not a stack of headings, labels and body styles.

### 1. Primary narrative voice

- Used for spatial sentences, highlighted sentences and the main prose in vertical sections.
- Large, sentence case and paced through line breaks, measure and whitespace rather than title hierarchy.
- One fluid type role across the experience; spatial placement may change its scale perceptually, but it remains the same voice.
- A highlighted sentence stays within the prose flow. It may gain weight, contrast or isolation, but it does not become a labelled section title.

### 2. Supporting detail voice

- Approximately the current 18px prose size.
- Used only inside vertical sections for factual context, project descriptions, dates, client or employer context, artefact captions and verification-sensitive details.
- Never used to carry the thesis, a transition or an essential part of the argument.

Studio-shell navigation and tool chrome may retain their existing typography. They are not part of the narrative hierarchy.

## Heading and labelling rules

- Remove constantly visible eyebrow labels and section numbers from the authored experience.
- Do not show noun headings such as **Profile**, **Trajectory**, **Practice** or **Next**.
- Keep semantic section names in the DOM for accessibility and authoring, but do not require them visually.
- Let the first meaningful sentence perform the orienting job normally given to a heading.
- Keep the progress indicator minimal or remove it if the continuous point-cloud transformation already communicates progression.

## Copy architecture

- Target **200–260 words** for the primary reading path, excluding optional detail prose and contact labels.
- Use **four to six spatial sentences** across the entire journey.
- Use **three substantial vertical prose passages**, usually 45–75 words each.
- Let “I” enter after the opening tension, then use it for origin, action and responsibility—not repeated positioning claims.
- Prefer one concrete tension and one specific decision over a list of disciplines.
- Keep client, role and career evidence adjacent to the claim it supports.
- Use the final invitation to continue the argument, not summarise the page.

## Factual boundary

Confirmed source material includes:

- the tension between clarity and character;
- poor simplification removing nuance, flavour and emotion;
- complexity being layered rather than denied;
- language and technology as formative interests;
- the expansion from visual form into behaviour, trust, products and systems;
- deep immersion, systematic thinking, creative direction, collaboration and prototyping;
- AI as an amplifier of judgement rather than its replacement;
- interest in consequential unresolved problems involving technology, trust and human behaviour.

Project-specific role, contribution, outcome and durability language remains subject to the verification sheet. S&P Global, Yoti, American Heart Association and McCann can guide working drafts, but public proof copy must be checked before publication.

## Failure modes

- A professional summary followed by a client list and future-interest list.
- A series of large slogans separated by small explanatory copy.
- Visible chapter labels that make the experience feel like a presentation deck.
- A biography that explains breadth but never reveals judgement.
- A point cloud that changes appearance without changing meaning.
- A perfect-grid ending that equates design with removing complexity.
- Literary language with no factual or experiential proof.
- Small prose carrying essential ideas because the primary narrative voice has been reserved for titles.

## Acceptance test

- The page makes one argument rather than presenting one profile.
- The reader encounters the premise before the biography.
- The middle explains a way of working, not a sequence of employers.
- The point cloud visibly moves the argument from complexity through structure to life.
- No visible eyebrow heading is needed to understand where the reader is.
- The experience works primarily with one large narrative voice and one supporting-detail voice.
- Small prose is confined to facts and descriptions in vertical sections.
- At least one sentence changes meaning because of the point-cloud state around it.
- The copy remains coherent in plain text and in reduced motion.
- The ending feels open, particular and invitational rather than like a list of desired responsibilities.
