# Final creative direction: About Me

## The decision

Build **a living editorial profile — the working mind, not the résumé** around one argument: **clarity without flattening**.

The page should make one concise professional story feel spatial and personal inside the existing `beck.fyi` shell. Large typography is the narrator. The existing flat-dot language moves through six meaningful states: cloud, signal, plane, pulse, wave and artefact. The environment begins in complexity, finds structure, then gains life. Three selected artefacts prove curiosity and give the page a human aftertaste.

This is not a portfolio grid, a career timeline, a client-logo wall, or a 3D tour. It is a readable vertical story whose optional interactions reward attention after the main point is already clear.

## Why this is the direction

The complete 40-site sample resolves the six questions set for the research:

| Research question | Evidence across the sample | Final decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What should an About page say? | The strongest pages position, prove and humanize. | Lead with one current thesis; follow it with proof and one human point of view. |
| How long can it be? | Median length is 354 words, but the most effective short reads cluster around 150–220 words. | Keep the required story to 175–220 words; place deeper detail behind optional reveals. |
| How can confidence stay human? | Claims work when a decision, client, scope or outcome immediately follows. | Use direct verbs and specific evidence; avoid superlatives and generic values. |
| Which interaction matters? | The best motion reinforces an already readable point. | Scroll is required; tap, pointer and drag only reveal or perturb secondary proof. |
| How can side work feel purposeful? | Personal artefacts are memorable when connected to a working principle. | Show two or three posters, experiments or small objects, each with a reason it informs the work. |
| What should `beck.fyi` do differently? | The research does not combine the site’s stable physical shell, expressive type and an authored point-cloud system. | Use the shared room as a stage, rather than adding a separate visual world. |

All 40 sites are included in `research-catalogue.txt` and `research-catalogue.json`; the deck deliberately spotlights six examples while its five-slide appendix indexes the full sample. The sample is balanced across 14 Awwwards, 13 Webby Awards and 13 CSS Design Awards selections.

## The page story

| Beat | Reader takeaway | Content | Behaviour |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Complexity | Reality contains context, contradiction and feeling | “The world isn’t flat. People aren’t either.” | Scroll enters a dense volumetric cloud; spatial phrases pass through one stable reading plane. |
| 2. Signal | Complexity is not the problem; confusion is | One short belief about attention and clarity | Local groups of dots align and hold inside the cloud. |
| 3. Trajectory | Graphic design trained the eye; product work expanded the problem | One selective career turn plus early poster artefacts | The environment withdraws into calm prose with a dot-raster poster stack at the edge. |
| 4. Structure | Alexander finds the system and makes it usable | Present practice, three specialties and selective client proof | The cloud resolves into an ordered dot plane. |
| 5. Heartbeat | Order alone can still feel dead | Motion, interaction, 3D and prototyping as ways to test life | One pulse travels through the grid and becomes a broad wave. |
| 6. Curiosity | Clarity can remain expressive and open | Two or three artefacts plus one honest aspiration | The wave settles into a living field with optional side interactions. |
| 7. Exit | The practice remains in motion | “Clarity, with a pulse.” | The field stays ordered but never completely still. |

## Content rules

- Core reading length: **175–220 words**.
- Use one dominant sentence per scene. Let the next sentence earn the next visual change.
- Name clients as evidence, not decoration. Three to six selective names are enough.
- Give every artefact a working connection: a method, fascination, experiment or point of view.
- Keep one voice: precise, thoughtful, lightly self-aware. Never defensive, inflated or overly polished.
- Treat LinkedIn details as source material only until the final interview verifies roles, dates, claims and aspirations.

## Interaction and accessibility contract

- Native vertical scroll is the only required navigation.
- Text remains readable before an animation completes; motion never holds information hostage.
- Tap/drag/pointer interactions reveal optional proof only and have a clear non-hover equivalent.
- Reduced motion preserves every narrative beat with a stable point-cloud state.
- Retain the existing shared shell, physical window, button bar, route transitions and circle language.

## Deliberate exclusions

- No second portfolio grid.
- No full career chronology.
- No logo wall as a substitute for contribution.
- No required hover, drag, sound or autoplay to understand the story.
- No thin helper lines, rings or unrelated visual gimmicks.
- No simulation that becomes the subject of the page rather than serving the narrative.

## What needs to be decided before copywriting

1. The one-sentence present-tense thesis.
2. The three trajectory turns and the client proof that genuinely supports them.
3. The three specialties, phrased as outcomes rather than disciplines.
4. The two or three side artefacts and why each belongs.
5. One honest next-step aspiration and the final light interaction.
