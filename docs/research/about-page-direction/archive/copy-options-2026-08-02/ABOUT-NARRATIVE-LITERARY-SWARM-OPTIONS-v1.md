# About narrative: literary swarm options v1

Status: review drafts

Prepared: 2026-08-02

These two scripts were developed from four independent editorial perspectives and then synthesised into two distinct narrative strategies. The named references supplied for the exercise were treated as broad editorial functions—conceptual clarity, contemporary first-person intimacy, commentary-style argument and calm inclusive rhetoric—not as voices to imitate.

Both versions retain the current order: About Me and description, early travelling titles, first prose block, client proof, bridge titles, six disciplines, second prose block, project impressions, closing titles and Get in Touch.

## Version 5 — Complexity has become ordinary

### About Me

> Complexity has become part of ordinary life. I’m Alex, a designer interested in helping people find a thoughtful way through it.

### Early travelling titles

> Complexity often hides inside an ordinary moment…

> …when a screen, choice or system quietly asks for trust.

### First prose block

> Hi, I’m Alex. I’m a designer at heart, and I notice complexity most when it looks harmless: a choice, a permission screen, a familiar tool that quietly asks us to trust a system we cannot see. I love design because it can make that hidden structure easier to meet without pretending it has disappeared.

> I learnt that lesson by changing direction. Computer Science showed me what technology could do; Communication Design in Mainz made me ask how another person would understand it. Visual language gave that question texture, emotion and consequence.

> Print taught me to shape attention. Digital work added behaviour. At Yoti, identity technology brought product, brand and trust into the same problem. My practice widened from there because the work itself kept crossing boundaries—into systems, motion, code, 3D and AI.

### Bridge travelling titles

> I didn’t set out to work across disciplines…

> …but kept following what each problem needed.

### Disciplines

1. **Product Design** — Shaping products around real human and organisational needs.
2. **Experience Design** — Connecting separate moments into an experience that makes sense.
3. **Art Direction** — Giving complex work a clear, distinctive visual point of view.
4. **Motion & 3D** — Using movement and space to explain changing relationships.
5. **Creative Engineering** — Using code to turn open questions into testable ideas.
6. **Parametric Systems** — Building flexible rules without making every outcome feel the same.

### Second prose block

> A brief usually presents the cleanest version of a problem. I try to find the messier, more truthful one underneath it. That means listening to people closest to the work, noticing compromises that have become invisible and asking what the brief left unsaid. Complexity is not a failure of organisation. Often, it means several real needs are competing for attention.

> Once I understand the tension, I make it tangible early. A sketch, prototype, motion study or piece of code gives everyone something real to challenge while choices remain open. I can set a direction, but I do not want to defend an idea simply because it began with me. In the best collaborations, ownership becomes less important: people feel heard, difficult decisions become shared decisions and I stay close enough to implementation to protect the reason behind the form.

> AI makes the tension between clarity and character harder to ignore. I use it to explore connections and prototype sooner, while keeping the purpose and final decisions human. Fast work can still be empty. I want the result to carry enough character that someone can feel it was made with care.

`[Project impressions]`

> That attention also changes how I judge an outcome. I care whether a person can tell what a system is asking, what will happen next and where their trust is being placed. The best result does not make complexity invisible. It gives people enough understanding to make a choice of their own.

### Closing travelling titles

> I don’t seek complexity for its own sake…

> …but the clarity that helps people act…

> …and make something new together.

### Get in Touch

> If something important resists an obvious answer, I’d like to hear about it.

## Version 6 — What the problem asks of me

### About Me

> Complexity is where I often find the human question. I’m Alex, a designer working where ideas, technology and everyday life meet.

### Early travelling titles

> Complex problems rarely belong to one discipline…

> …and that is exactly why I keep following them.

### First prose block

> Hi, I’m Alex. I’m a designer at heart, and I still get a thrill when a difficult idea becomes something another person can see, question or use. Once it is visible, it no longer belongs only to me; the conversation can start.

> I began by studying Computer Science because technology felt full of possibility. But speaking several languages had already made me notice how much meaning depends on context: the same thought can feel obvious to one person and completely foreign to another. Communication Design in Mainz gave me a place to bring those interests together. I could work with language, images and systems while staying close to the person trying to understand them.

> My early agency work was rooted in print and culture. It taught me how much a typeface, an image or a small shift in composition can change the way something is received. Digital work complicated the picture in a useful way. Interfaces had to behave, not merely look resolved. Working beside developers drew me into motion, code and systems; moving to London for Yoti showed me that product, brand and trust can become one problem when people are being asked to understand unfamiliar technology.

### Bridge travelling titles

> I stopped asking where a problem belonged…

> …and started asking what it needed.

### Disciplines

1. **Product Design** — Shaping products around real human and organisational needs.
2. **Experience Design** — Connecting separate moments into an experience that makes sense.
3. **Art Direction** — Giving complex work a clear, distinctive visual point of view.
4. **Motion & 3D** — Using movement and space to explain changing relationships.
5. **Creative Engineering** — Using code to turn open questions into testable ideas.
6. **Parametric Systems** — Building flexible rules without making every outcome feel the same.

### Second prose block

> I like beginning without pretending I know the answer. I listen to the people nearest the problem and look for the assumption hiding inside the brief. Then I make something—a sketch, prototype, motion study or piece of code—because a tangible idea gives the team something honest to discuss.

> When I lead, I try to make the direction clear enough to help and open enough to challenge. I want collaborators to recognise their thinking in the result. I also stay close when the work is built, because implementation is where small choices either preserve the idea or weaken it.

> AI has become part of that practice. I use it to explore, connect ideas and prototype, but not to avoid judgement. The easier it becomes to produce a plausible answer, the more carefully I ask who it serves, what it changes and where its character comes from.

`[Project impressions]`

> The same curiosity follows me home. It turns up in music, Lego and independent games, and in a daily irritation that refuses to stay small—like my attempt to rethink the iOS keyboard. I’m also drawn to unfinished questions around trust, privacy and robotics, especially before the answers become conventional.

### Closing travelling titles

> I don’t know the next question…

> …only that it should feel unfamiliar…

> …and give us something new to make.

### Get in Touch

> If an unfamiliar problem keeps following you, I’d love to hear about it.
