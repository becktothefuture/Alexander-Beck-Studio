# About narrative closing exploration

Status: superseded editorial record

Prepared: 2026-08-03

This record preserves the closing routes tested after the curiosity-first spoken draft. None of the rejected wording is active copy.

## What the ending must do

- Resolve the six disciplines as one practice.
- Say that the disciplines create something new by changing one another.
- Avoid presenting Alex as unusually important or visionary.
- Avoid turning the ending into a statement about collaboration or other people.
- Remain short enough for the three existing travelling-title beats.
- Let the final call to action continue the idea rather than reverting to a generic contact label.

## Superseded routes

### Abstract and self-important

> Each discipline brings a different part of the answer…
>
> …and it’s in the sum of those parts…
>
> …that we have the chance to create something new.

Rejected because “part of the answer” and “we have the chance” sounded generic and inflated.

### Too basic

> I don’t see these as separate jobs…
>
> …they’re different parts of how I work…
>
> …and together they let me make things I couldn’t make with one alone.

Rejected because it described combining skills without saying anything distinctive about what the combination does.

### Too long and poorly connected

> Complex problems don’t arrive labelled ‘product’, ‘brand’ or ‘technology’…
>
> …so I follow them across those boundaries and use whatever helps…
>
> …which often leads to something new that doesn’t fit neatly into any one of them.

Rejected because the thought was too long and did not resolve cleanly into the finale.

### Wrongly centred on other people

> That’s why I work across disciplines…
>
> …not to collect more labels, but to bring different ideas into the same piece of work…
>
> …and make something new with people who can see the parts I can’t.

Rejected because the ending became a statement about collaboration rather than creating something new from the disciplines themselves.

## Final five directions considered

### 1

> Each discipline offers a different way into the work…
>
> …none of them has to determine where it ends…
>
> …together, they can lead to something new.

### 2

> The disciplines themselves may be familiar…
>
> …the way they are combined does not have to be…
>
> …and that combination can create something new.

### 3

> Different disciplines bring different kinds of knowledge…
>
> …the work changes when those forms of knowledge meet…
>
> …and becomes something new in its own right.

### 4

> Product, communication, code and motion each have their own language…
>
> …the interesting part is what happens when those languages mix…
>
> …and the work begins to speak in a new one.

### 5 — selected direction

> Different disciplines don’t simply add up…
>
> …they change one another as the work develops…
>
> …until the combination becomes something new.

Direction 5 was selected because it contains the actual point: multidisciplinary work is transformative rather than additive.

## Finale refinement

The generic Get in Touch title was rejected. The selected ending was adjusted to avoid repeating “something new” in consecutive frames:

> Different disciplines don’t simply add up…
>
> …they change one another as the work develops…
>
> …until the combination becomes something of its own.

The final interface title then completes the thought:

> Let’s make something new
