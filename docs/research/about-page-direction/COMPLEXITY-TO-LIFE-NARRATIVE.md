# About Me: complexity to life

## Status

This direction supersedes the literal-room routes in `ORCHESTRATION-IDEATION.md`.

## The central idea

**Clarity without flattening.**

The world is complex because people, organisations and experiences contain context, contradictions, systems, emotion and history. Good design should not remove that richness. It should make it possible to understand and use.

The page makes this argument spatially:

**chaos → signal → structure → life**

The reader enters a dense volumetric cloud. Meaning appears while they move through it. The cloud gradually resolves into an ordered plane. But order alone becomes too still and too flat. A pulse enters the system. The plane lifts, bends and begins to breathe. Structure remains, but it now carries emotion, curiosity and human response.

That transformation mirrors Alexander’s work: understand complexity, find the system inside it, make it clear, then give it character.

## One material, six states

Everything is built from the existing flat dots and site palette. Visual variety comes from behaviour, density and depth rather than introducing unrelated materials.

| State | Visual behaviour | Narrative meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cloud | Dense, volumetric, irregular and difficult to read at once | The real world contains more than a flat interface can show. |
| Signal | Small regions align, brighten or briefly hold their position | Attention finds useful meaning inside complexity. |
| Plane | Dots resolve into an ordered, grid-based surface | Design gives information structure and makes action possible. |
| Pulse | A single rhythmic compression travels through the grid | Systems need human response, not only order. |
| Wave | The plane gains depth, softness and changing amplitude | Structure becomes expressive without losing clarity. |
| Artefact | Dots assemble into poster fragments and experimental images | Personal history and curiosity become concrete evidence. |

The point cloud is therefore not present in the same way throughout. It changes state with the argument.

## Story and experience beats

### Beat 1 — Complexity is not the enemy

**Experience:** The reader begins outside a dense tunnel of dots. The field is beautiful but difficult to comprehend. Scroll moves the camera inward.

**Spatial type:**

- THE WORLD ISN’T FLAT.
- PEOPLE AREN’T EITHER.
- COMPLEXITY IS NOT THE ENEMY.

The phrases exist at different depths. Each becomes readable only when it reaches the camera’s stable reading plane.

**Narrative job:** Establish the belief before introducing Alexander.

### Beat 2 — Finding signal

**Experience:** Inside the cloud, local groups of points begin to align. A few colors hold together. The tunnel remains complex, but it is no longer random.

**Spatial type:**

- CONFUSION IS.
- CLARITY BEGINS WITH ATTENTION.

**Narrative job:** Make the distinction between simplifying reality and making it understandable.

### Beat 3 — The first quiet surface

**Experience:** The environment thins and fades. The fixed camera gives way to a calm editorial section. Prose appears line by line as it crosses one consistent reading threshold. A few isolated dots continue moving slowly in depth.

**Content:** Graphic-design origins, followed by the move into product and experience design.

**Artefact detail:** A narrow stack of early posters appears at one edge. At rest, each poster is translated into the site’s coloured-dot raster. Tap or flick lets one piece settle into clearer focus with a one-line note about what it taught Alexander.

**Narrative job:** Connect the philosophy to a real trajectory without turning into a CV.

### Beat 4 — Structure emerges

**Experience:** The prose dissolves back into dots. The cloud opens and resolves into a large ordered plane extending through space. Three broad regions represent the recurring work: product clarity, visual character and interaction.

**Proof:** Selective client names—American Heart Association, S&P Global, Bentley, Yoti and SunExpress—appear as quiet evidence within the field, never as a logo wall.

**Spatial type:**

- FIND THE STRUCTURE.
- MAKE IT USABLE.

**Narrative job:** Show that clarity, systems and delivery are concrete parts of the work.

### Beat 5 — The problem with perfect order

**Experience:** For a moment, the grid becomes completely still. It is precise and legible, but intentionally lifeless. The pause should feel slightly uncomfortable.

**Spatial type:**

- BUT ORDER IS NOT ENOUGH.

**Narrative job:** Prevent the story from ending with “complexity becomes a grid.” This is the conceptual turn.

### Beat 6 — Give the system a heartbeat

**Experience:** One point expands and contracts. Its rhythm propagates through neighboring dots. The grid becomes a pulse, then a broad wave. Depth, color and motion return without destroying the underlying structure.

The heartbeat should feel physical but restrained: one clear pulse, a brief settling period and slight variation. It must not resemble an audio visualizer or medical monitor.

**Spatial type:**

- GIVE IT A PULSE.
- LEAVE ROOM FOR CURIOSITY.

**Narrative job:** Express Alexander’s combination of product thinking, art direction, motion, 3D and creative engineering.

### Beat 7 — A living field

**Experience:** The wave calms into an open spatial field. It remains ordered, but never static. Small dot-built artefacts gather along the sides. They can be explored without interrupting vertical scroll.

**Content:** One aspiration: to keep making digital experiences feel more spatial, responsive and human.

**Closing type:**

- CLARITY, WITH A PULSE.

**Narrative job:** End on a point of view and an open direction, not a claim of mastery.

## Page rhythm

| Range | Mode | Primary content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–20% | Volumetric traversal | Complexity and the opening belief |
| 20–32% | Signal inside the cloud | Attention and clarity |
| 32–48% | Editorial prose | Graphic-design origin and selective trajectory |
| 48–65% | Grid-space traversal | Present practice, specialties and client proof |
| 65–74% | Deliberate stillness | The limitation of order alone |
| 74–90% | Pulse and wave | Motion, character and human response |
| 90–100% | Open field and artefacts | Curiosity, aspiration and exit |

The choreography alternates intensity:

**deep space → quiet prose → ordered space → stillness → living space**

## Second story draft

> Digital experiences are often asked to make complicated things feel simple. But simple can become flat.
>
> Clarity should make complexity understandable without removing what makes it human: context, character, movement and surprise.
>
> I learned that first through graphic design. Type, image and rhythm can turn noise into meaning without erasing its energy. That led me into products and services, where the same principle has to work at a larger scale: across users, systems, constraints and decisions.
>
> Today I work across product design, experience design, art direction, motion, 3D and creative engineering. For organisations including the American Heart Association, S&P Global, Bentley, Yoti and SunExpress, the task has often been similar: find the structure, make it usable, then give it character.
>
> Because structure is only the beginning. A perfect grid can still feel lifeless.
>
> Prototypes let me test what brings it back: how an interaction responds, how motion guides attention, how a system holds together, and how a brand feels when someone actually uses it.
>
> I want to keep making digital experiences with depth, clarity and a pulse. Experiences that make sense—and still leave room for curiosity.

**Draft length:** 186 words.

| “Today I work across…” | Editorial prose leading into the grid |

| “Because order alone…” | Still grid and the conceptual turn |
| “The work comes alive…” through the prototype sentence | Heartbeat and wave sequence |

The large spatial phrases do not need to repeat the prose word for word. They act as chapter statements; the prose supplies nuance and evidence.

## Tone check

The second draft follows the agreed rules in `ABOUT-TONE-OF-VOICE.md`:

- belief first, biography second;
- direct language rather than self-praise;
- concrete disciplines and clients as proof;
- one selective trajectory rather than a complete chronology;
- an aspiration rather than a grand conclusion;
- human warmth through specificity, not inflated adjectives.

The claims and client list are drawn from current portfolio material. Roles, dates and contribution language must still be verified before public copy is approved.

## Plan for the next phase

### 1. Lock the argument

Choose the final thesis and conceptual turn:

- **Clarity without flattening** — recommended overall idea.
- **Complexity is not the enemy. Confusion is.** — strongest opening argument.
- **Structure needs a heartbeat.** — strongest midpoint turn.
- **Clarity, with a pulse.** — strongest closing line.

### 2. Verify the personal facts

Confirm the graphic-design origin, three career turns, five client names, current practice description and future aspiration. Keep the final interview focused on what the visual story actually needs.

### 3. Create a twelve-frame storyboard

Draw entry, readable midpoint and exit frames for the cloud, grid, heartbeat and wave. Add two editorial frames and two artefact states. Include desktop, mobile and reduced-motion annotations from the start.

### 4. Test four isolated behaviours

1. Spatial phrase passing through the camera’s reading plane.
2. Dense cloud resolving into a stable dot grid.
3. One pulse propagating through the grid and becoming a wave.
4. A dot-raster poster stack that can be explored without trapping scroll.

Keep each test only if the meaning is clear without an explanation beside it.

### 5. Build an animatic before a browser prototype

Combine the kept tests into a simple timed scroll film. Judge pacing, reading time and emotional progression before deciding how to source or generate the spatial assets.

### 6. Define the implementation system

Only after the animatic is approved, specify the shared camera, scroll mapping, text layers, dot-state transitions, mobile simplification, reduced motion and performance budgets. No production implementation begins before this gate.

## Decisions for the next conversation

3. Which early posters best prove the graphic-design origin?
4. Should the ending feel peaceful and resolved, or curious and still evolving?
