# About Me — story-led draft v3

## Purpose

This is a text-first working draft for manual refinement, not public-ready copy or an implementation brief. It returns to the original brief: make the About page feel like a short, living editorial story that shows how Alexander gives complex work form and character — not a CV presented with animation.

The first eight beats from the prior storyboard remain intact. This draft changes the prose architecture and replaces the final visual movement.

## Narrative — one paragraph per beat

### 01 — Promise

> I help ambitious teams shape complex ideas into clear, human and emotionally compelling experiences.

**Role in the page:** The first and only immediate positioning statement. It should be visible before movement becomes demanding.

### 02 — Enter complexity

> The problems worth working on rarely arrive in neat pieces. They bring people with different needs, systems that affect one another and decisions with no obvious owner. I do not want to smooth that away. I want to understand what is really going on.

**Role in the page:** The camera moves into the cloud. Complexity is not cast as a problem to destroy; it is material that needs attention.

### 03 — Personal lens

> That instinct began with language and technology, then found a visual form. I was drawn to the way a word, an interaction or a small shift in rhythm can change what an idea means. Over time, design became less about arranging things and more about helping a whole experience make sense.

**Role in the page:** The cloud opens into the calm field. This explains the background as a way of seeing, not a career chronology.

### 04 — How the work moves

> That is how I tend to work now: I get close to the detail, give the important parts a form people can recognise, then make it tangible enough for a team to test, question and improve together.

**Role in the page:** This carries the three-part way of working without making it sound like a branded framework. In the design it may break gently across three reading moments: **get close**, **give form**, **make tangible**.

### 05 — The available materials

> The tools change with the problem: product design, experience design, art direction, motion and 3D, creative engineering, parametric systems. I do not treat them as separate answers. They are different ways of giving one idea the right shape, rhythm and degree of interaction.

**Role in the page:** The field becomes a flat grid and the six disciplines are highlighted. The names stay exact and literal; they are evidence of breadth, not six mini-brands.

### 06 — The turn from order to life

> Once something is clear, there is still another question: does it have a pulse? A perfect system can be efficient and still feel remote. The work comes alive when structure leaves room for character — through movement, tactility, a useful surprise or a visible human decision.

**Role in the page:** The labels recede. The entire grid, rather than six separate regions, begins to respond. This is the conceptual hinge and visual climax.

### 07 — Direction of travel

> That is the space I want to keep working in: ambitious, unresolved problems where technology has to earn people’s trust and where the solution may need a new approach. I want collaborators to feel heard, understood and cared for along the way.

**Role in the page:** The reader travels through the responsive field. It is the future-facing invitation before the CTA; it does not repeat the professional promise.

### 08 — Epilogue

> Bring me something unresolved.

**Role in the page:** The field resolves into the larger bust. This is a holding moment, not the payoff of a metaphor. It gives the reader a face, a quiet pause and a clear next action.

## Why this is a better three-part method

The three verbs are the practical form of the whole story, not a separate doctrine:

| Story | Working impulse | Visual state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Complexity deserves attention. | Get close. | Enter the cloud. |
| Attention turns into something legible. | Give form. | Calm field becomes a grid. |
| Legibility must still feel human. | Make tangible. | Grid responds, moves and becomes a shared experience. |

This removes “find the organising idea” as the page’s central claim. It remains implicit in **give form**, where it is earned by the visible transition from density to structure.

## Revised final visual movement — grid to bust

**The key change:** the six disciplines do not generate six literal features in a world. They activate the reader’s understanding of the available materials, then dissolve back into one field. From that point, the grid itself is the subject.

| Frame | What happens | Why it belongs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 09 — Release | The six labels soften away. Their dots return to the same scale and material as every other dot. A single slow phase shift crosses the grid: spacing compresses and releases in wide bands. | The disciplines are absorbed into one practice, without being reduced to a visual allegory. |
| 10 — Travel | The camera lowers from the bird’s-eye grid and travels forward along the first rising wave. The plane becomes a responsive field: broad undulations, changing density and small local variations, with no objects placed on top. | This makes the difference between a static system and a living one physically felt. |
| 11 — Perspective | The waves gain enough depth to become a single moving point volume. As the camera arcs around it, the field opens around a calm circular absence and begins a slow shared orbital rhythm. | The reader feels coherence and the possibility of a final gathering without seeing a premature face reveal. |
| 12 — Epilogue | The camera settles. The moving volume releases and the same dots finish gathering into a large, recognisable revolving bust. A small number continue a slow orbit at its edge, preserving the sense that the work is alive. | The bust arrives as the final stable state of the material already travelled through. |

### Guardrails

- No monoliths, airborne formations, “world-building” props or six-to-one visual mapping.
- No literal morph cut. The transition is a change of camera angle, density and dot position over a single continuous field.
- The visual climax is Frame 11’s responsive field. Frame 12 is deliberately quieter.
- Keep the six highlighted dots and their labels only in the expertise beat. Once the labels leave, do not reintroduce them as coloured characters or causes.
- Let the bust be larger than before: roughly half of the studio-window height, with generous CTA space.

## Working edit notes

- Keep Beat 01 as the opening unless a later, more personal opening earns its replacement.
- If the primary read needs shortening, cut from Beat 02 first, then Beat 05; retain Beat 06 because it is the thesis the visuals can prove.
- Keep project names out of the primary line until their claims are verified. They work better as optional support after the relevant statement.
- Read the full text aloud. It should sound like an intelligent person describing the work, not a studio manifesto.
