# About Me orchestration ideation

> **Status:** Superseded by `COMPLEXITY-TO-LIFE-NARRATIVE.md`. This file is retained as an exploration record.

## The decision we are making

How should a 175–220 word professional story alternate between spatial typography, readable prose and explorable artefacts so that every visual moment explains something about Alexander’s practice?

The page should feel like travelling through a mind and a body of work—not watching a point-cloud demo.

## The design axes

| Axis | Low end | High end | What it controls |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Environment | Abstract field | Recognisable place | Whether the spatial world feels conceptual or autobiographical. |
| Continuity | One transforming room | Several distinct spaces | Whether the page feels seamless or chaptered. |
| Type depth | Editorial surface | Type placed in space | How much reading depends on movement toward and past the camera. |
| Artefact presence | Occasional evidence | Active personal cabinet | How strongly posters and experiments shape the story. |
| Motion intensity | Quiet transitions | Cinematic traversal | How often scroll produces a major spatial event. |
| Production burden | Existing runtime and 2.5D assets | New scans and authored 3D scenes | What must be captured, modelled or purchased. |

## Three possible routes

### Route A — The Working Room

One recognisable studio, home workspace or corridor is captured as a point cloud. The camera moves through the same environment throughout the page. Type occupies thresholds, walls and depth planes; posters and objects are located where they would plausibly exist.

**What it means:** the reader is entering the place where the work happens.

**Strengths:** personal, coherent, achievable with one strong LiDAR capture, and naturally suited to the existing Spatial Scan runtime.

**Risk:** the room can become too literal or visually repetitive. If the scan is not beautiful, the entire concept inherits that weakness.

**Best when:** intimacy and autobiography matter more than strong chapter contrast.

### Route B — Three Rooms of Practice

The story crosses three distinct spaces: an archive for graphic-design origins, a structured system-space for product and interface work, and an open experimental space for curiosity and the future. Calm prose bridges the rooms.

**What it means:** each environment embodies a different phase or mode of practice.

**Strengths:** the clearest narrative symbolism and the richest alternation of rhythm.

**Risk:** three unrelated models can feel like a showreel. It also creates the highest asset and performance burden.

**Best when:** we can author every environment from the story rather than choosing spaces because they look impressive.

### Route C — The Field Becomes Things

There is no literal room. One field of flat circles continually reorganises into spatial type, broad planes, image silhouettes, clusters and openings. Real posters and artefacts interrupt the field as crisp photographic objects.

**What it means:** the same design material can become communication, systems and experiences.

**Strengths:** closest to the existing `beck.fyi` language, most controllable, and least dependent on sourcing a perfect scan.

**Risk:** it may feel less human and less grounded in reality. Without strong content logic, morphing particles become decorative.

**Best when:** visual coherence and performance control matter more than a recognisable environment.

## Recommended route — The Studio of Evidence

Combine the strongest parts of all three routes: use **one real environment as an anchor**, then let the same point material transform into two authored, less literal spatial states. Real artefacts remain crisp and tactile throughout.

The four major animation moments form one trajectory:

1. **Threshold — entering the practice**
   A real corridor, doorway or studio threshold appears as a point cloud. The opening professional thesis sits in its depth. The reader moves toward and through the sentence.

2. **Archive — where the eye was trained**
   The environment dissolves into an archive of poster planes and printed fragments. This is not another point-cloud room: the posters remain real images, while dots provide depth, dust and transitions. A partial stack appears at the edge before the reader is invited to flick through it.

3. **System — how the practice works now**
   The dots reorganise into broad layered planes, apertures and clusters that suggest interface depth without copying literal UI screens. The specialties are experienced as three spatial layers: clarity, systems and interaction.

4. **Open studio — what remains unfinished**
   The system opens into a calmer, less bounded field. Two or three personal artefacts sit along the sides and can be brought forward. The page ends with one playful physical object and one honest aspiration.

This gives the spatial environment a meaning: **the reader moves from entering the practice, through its visual origins, into its present system of working, and finally toward what Alexander wants to explore next.**

## Recommended page rhythm

| Page range | Section | Reading mode | Visual job |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–12% | Position | Spatial headline | Establish the thesis and the real-room anchor. |
| 12–30% | Selective trajectory | Editorial prose | Three short career turns reveal line by line; the point cloud recedes almost completely. |
| 30–48% | Graphic-design origins | Archive traversal | Posters and print fragments make the origin tangible. |
| 48–66% | Present specialties and beliefs | Editorial prose | Three outcome-led strengths; occasional dot clusters mark transitions, not sentences. |
| 66–84% | How the work becomes systems | Abstract spatial traversal | Layered depth embodies structure, interaction and clarity. |
| 84–100% | Curiosity, artefacts and next | Open cabinet / closing scene | Side work, aspiration and a light final interaction. |

The rhythm is therefore:

**space → prose → space → prose → space → cabinet**

The two prose sections are essential. They prevent the page from becoming one continuous effect and make the next spatial moment feel earned.

## Three typography behaviours

### 1. Spatial headline

Use for only two or three decisive statements.

- A phrase begins small, distant, softly blurred and transparent.
- It reaches one stable reading plane with full opacity and no blur.
- It then grows beyond the viewport, softens and disappears past the reader.
- Move semantic phrases of roughly two to five words, not every individual word.
- Give each phrase enough scroll distance to be read before it moves again.

Word-by-word travel is seductive, but using it for whole sentences would make reading feel like work. Reserve individual-word depth for one deliberately rhythmic line, if the copy supports it.

### 2. Editorial prose

Use for trajectory, specialties and beliefs.

- Text occupies a stable DOM plane at approximately the calmer Contact-page title/body scale.
- Lines reveal as they cross a consistent reading threshold.
- Completed lines remain visible long enough to read as a paragraph.
- The background becomes quieter and flatter; the point field may thin to a few slow depth cues.

### 3. Artefact label

Use for posters, experiments and personal objects.

- One short label identifies the object.
- One sentence explains why it belongs in the story or what it taught Alexander.
- Labels remain attached to the object when it is moved or expanded.

## The point cloud has four jobs—not one

1. **Place:** describe the threshold environment.
2. **Transition:** dissolve a spatial chapter into a quiet editorial surface.
3. **Structure:** reform into broad depth layers during the systems chapter.
4. **Atmosphere:** settle around real artefacts without turning them into particles.

The cloud should disappear almost entirely during longer prose. Its absence is part of the choreography.

## Artefact strategy — seed early, resolve late

Do not save every personal object for a final gallery.

- In the archive chapter, show the first poster stack partially cropped at one side. It proves the graphic-design origin at the exact moment it is mentioned.
- Let the user flick through three to five pieces, but keep one featured piece already readable without interaction.
- Each item gets a single causal caption: what changed, what was learned, or what fascination it reveals.
- At the end, reassemble the selected poster stack with two newer experiments or objects. The closing cabinet then feels like the accumulation of the story rather than an unrelated showcase.

On touch, tapping should advance or expand the stack. On pointer devices, a horizontal flick or drag can add tactility. Neither behavior may trap vertical page scroll.

## What the environments could physically be

### Threshold capture

Best source: a real corridor, doorway, studio corner or domestic threshold that Alexander can scan. It should contain strong foreground, middle-ground and background planes rather than lots of small clutter.

### Archive construction

Best source: real poster images arranged as 2.5D planes, optionally combined with a scan of a desk, print drawer or physical stack. This scene does not need to be a full LiDAR room.

### System construction

Best source: an authored procedural space made from broad surfaces and point clusters. It should feel like depth inside an interface—not a futuristic tunnel, wireframe city or literal exploded UI.

### Open studio

Best source: the threshold environment gradually stripped back until only a few planes, dots and real artefacts remain. Reusing the anchor space gives the ending continuity.

## Guardrails

- Every major motion moment must answer a new part of the story.
- Do not use four visually unrelated 3D assets.
- Do not convert posters or photographs into point clouds; their material contrast is valuable.
- Do not keep large text moving while it is supposed to be read.
- Do not make the spatial text the only accessible version of the copy.
- Do not allow an artefact interaction to block vertical scroll.
- Preserve the site’s flat-circle language; avoid helper lines, wireframes and generic sci-fi tunnels.
- Design reduced-motion states at the storyboard stage, not after implementation.

## The next ideation loop

Use one mutable storyboard rather than designing the whole page at once.

### Fixed preview set

- Desktop: six checkpoints, one per page section.
- Mobile: the same six checkpoints in the same narrative order.
- Motion strip: entry, readable midpoint and exit state for each of the four major moments.
- Reduced motion: one stable frame for every section.

### Evaluation rubric

Score each experiment from 1–5 on:

- narrative meaning;
- immediate legibility;
- rhythm and contrast;
- distinctiveness within `beck.fyi`;
- human warmth;
- mobile translation;
- implementation and performance realism.

Any experiment that loses essential copy, traps scrolling, requires hover, or looks unrelated to the circle language is discarded regardless of its score.

### Bounded experiments

1. **Spatial-type test:** one opening phrase moving from distant blur through a stable reading plane and past the camera.
2. **Breathing test:** one transition from the point-cloud threshold into a quiet prose section.
3. **Archive test:** one three-poster stack with a safe scroll/flick relationship.
4. **System-space test:** three broad depth layers made from the existing dot material.
5. **Sequence test:** combine only the kept results into a six-section storyboard.

## Decisions for the next discussion

1. Should the real anchor environment feel domestic, studio-like or architecturally neutral?
2. Is the professional trajectory best expressed as **threshold → archive → system → open studio**, or should the archive arrive later?
3. Which one statement deserves the most cinematic spatial-type treatment?
4. Which three to five existing posters or small projects are strong enough to form the first archive stack?
5. Should the closing interaction feel tactile and physical, or quiet and contemplative?

## Reusable creative brief

Design a scroll-led About Me page inside the existing `beck.fyi` shell. The reader travels from a real point-cloud threshold through a graphic archive, an authored system-space and an open studio. Alternate cinematic spatial headlines with calm editorial prose. Use the point field only for place, transition, structure and atmosphere; keep posters and artefacts crisp and tactile. Every visual change must explain the professional trajectory, remain readable without motion and preserve native vertical scrolling.
