# About Me — culmination and travel implementation plan

Date: 30 August 2026

Status: **creative direction rejected in subsequent review; not approved for implementation**.

See [the creative-standard reset and action plan](ABOUT-V2-AWARD-STANDARD-RESET-2026-08-30.md). Preserve this document as a record of the investigated constraints, not as authorisation to build Open Assembly. The three-surface concept and its prescribed lift/tilt loop did not establish a sufficiently specific idea or earned payoff.

Audience: prospective clients, hiring teams and collaborators meeting Alex through About Me.

## 1. Decision and scope

The present ending is legible, but it is not yet a culmination. It continues the method corridor instead of revealing a new subject.

Build **The Open Assembly**: one asymmetric kinetic sculpture made from three broad folded point surfaces. The camera emerges from the banks, slows into a composed final view and stops. The three surfaces continue a coordinated lift-and-tilt movement below the invitation. The change is from travelling through an environment to standing before one complete, living work.

The payoff is that different ways of thinking work together. The celebration comes from the new composition, the opening of space and coherent movement. It adds **zero words, zero text fields and no mandatory viewing delay**.

This plan supplements the [previous refinement plan](ABOUT-V2-JURY-REFINEMENT-PLAN-2026-08-30.md). It supersedes these specific parts:

- The restriction against new terminal geometry and the corridor-only final image.
- The fixed old final camera and the assumption that a 0.80-WU gap alone solves arrival.
- The proposal to lower the mobile invitation group. The new composition reserves the lower stage for the sculpture.
- The old implementation order. Prove the ending first, then integrate and remeasure the full narrative.

The earlier world is protected. This is not permission to rebuild the route, restore the retired lens scene, thin the whole point population, change the shell or introduce another renderer. The source camera, roll and FOV remain authored in Blender; only bounded terminal staging may change. Earlier timing can be reallocated where necessary for smooth travel, without moving earlier landmarks or prolonging the shortened gate event.

The two closing titles carry the reveal. There is no new silent approach chapter, particle explosion or additional end title. Visitors who arrive quickly still receive the complete composition and usable actions. They never have to wait for a special pose or a full motion cycle.

### The subject

Author three broad, open-ended surfaces: one rounded turn, one softened angular fold and one low saddle-like wave. Arrange them on an asymmetric, staggered axis, at different heights and depths. Reuse the earlier curve/corner/landscape language without literally collecting previous scenery into a pile.

Each part must remain shaped and separate in a small still. They form one open rising composition, not three orbiting petals. No closed loops, halo, sphere, thin ribbons, pedestal, floating furniture or supporting confetti. Three is a compositional choice, not a claim about the six disciplines.

Keep the existing opaque circular point material and shared palette schedule. Curvature and dispersed points describe the surfaces; do not turn them into continuous painted skins or add glow. Movement, not extra density or independent colour cycling, supplies the energy.

### Composition on both viewports

Use the same hierarchy on portrait and desktop: **invitation above, complete sculpture below**. Start the blockout with these envelopes, measured inside the usable studio window:

- Title, supporting line and both actions: approximately the upper 12–42%.
- Sculpture, including its entire motion and point radii: approximately 50–84% vertically; inside the central 80% of portrait width. Desktop should remain a compact whole subject, not new side walls.
- Keep a real clear gap between the lowest action/focus outline and the highest moving point. Preserve Button Bar, Utility Rail and short-portrait clearances.

These are initial composition targets, not fixed CSS values or proven projections. Solve them with the terminal object's dimensions, placement and authored camera. Do not independently offset the object in each browser profile or shrink the text/buttons to accommodate it.

Check the complete motion envelope, not only a favourable frame. If one plane vanishes on mobile, change its shape, spacing or permitted tilt. Do not reduce the earlier world to buy space. Banks and sculpture must never collapse into one dense overlapping view during approach or reversal.

## 4. Camera: one distance schedule, then a true brake

### Source findings

The verified export contains 3,600 camera samples, approximately 1,313.968 path units and a repeated held endpoint. The final 324 segments have neither translation nor rotation. Progress 0.91–1 is **not unused travel**. The final visible model is `GN_RESPONSIVE_LATTICE`; there is no hidden unique object for a fog adjustment to reveal.

Changing a terminal curve can alter its total length and shift earlier objects attached by normalised curve fractions. Freeze earlier semantic camera poses and object transforms. Preserve or explicitly rebind those anchors when changing the tail. A matching object name or control-point count does not prove preservation.

### A single progression owner

Keep the existing timeline and Journey Map. Use this chain:

`effective story position → compiled path distance → cached arc-length lookup → authored position + quaternion`

Build the cumulative-distance lookup once per verified camera asset. Retain original sample indices and interpolate position and orientation from the same segment. Do not fit a new browser curve, alter the authored roll, or add an independent orientation ease. Repeated held samples must not create division by zero or invented travel. This follows the distinction between raw curve parameters and distance-based sampling in the [Three.js curve documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/Curve.html).

After fonts/layout settle, compile one monotone distance-versus-story schedule. Adjacent travel intervals share a derivative rather than each receiving an independent ease-in/out. Reading spans may move more slowly; scene-to-scene travel should have a consistent cruise feel. Check projected motion and angular changes too: equal world distance does not guarantee equal perceived speed.

Distance and scroll allocation have to agree. The current terrain-to-gate approach travels about 209.94 units in 0.46 story-WU; the gate itself travels a similar distance in 1.10 WU. Matching that gate cruise would need roughly 1.10 WU for the approach. Reallocate the existing passage budget or permissible cue bindings after the copy edit; interpolation alone cannot erase this difference. Preserve the short gate event and keep the overall page shorter than baseline.

### Terminal brake

Choose the new final path length, incoming speed and available closing interval together. A suitable decreasing-velocity profile is:

```text
u = progress through the braking interval, from 0 to 1
v(u) = v_in × [1 − (6u^5 − 15u^4 + 10u^3)]
distance(u) = v_in × U × [u − 2.5u^4 + 3u^5 − u^6]
total braking distance D = v_in × U / 2
```

Here `U` is story-WU, not seconds. It enters at the incoming speed, never accelerates during the brake and reaches zero smoothly. It is not a generic ease-in/out position curve that starts by accelerating. Match the incoming slope to the compatibility equation rather than scaling an arbitrary curve to the endpoint.

The old final approach is about **32.521 path units**. Using its preceding average speed would require about **6.28–6.93 WU** for this brake. The prior short-gap test leaves only 2.30–2.32 WU and would require roughly three times that entrance speed. Therefore, **shorten/reposition the physical terminal approach** as part of the new blockout. Do not lengthen the page by six screens or disguise a speed-up with damping. These old-scene figures diagnose the constraint; they are not dimensions for the new sculpture.

The previous 0.80-WU gap is only a candidate breath budget. Reveal and braking also use the existing closing-title spans. Measure the complete revised page; its length must remain below the current 26.452069-WU mobile / 27.152033-WU desktop baseline, with no lost reading interval. Visitors still control how long they read. A fixed scroll-rate test can certify deceleration; the page must not seize input to force a running time.

### Input, settling and seeks

Reuse Lenis' effective scroll coordinate on eligible desktop and native effective scroll on touch. Pass the current 420ms Cartesian/quaternion follower through for this distance-profile route. Position and orientation then come from the same authored rail sample, without an extra filter cutting across bends or putting camera and text on different clocks.

Retire `steadycamResponseMs` from this profile's live controls; retain old saved values only as documented parse compatibility. Keep the existing desktop `scrollSmoothing` / Track glide control, and identify native scrolling as browser-managed. Do not leave a slider claiming to change a bypassed filter. These are profile-specific changes, not a rewrite of the site's other scroll systems.

Test touch control before accepting this policy. If native-device proof demonstrates a need for additional settling, use one scalar rail-coordinate follower that **replaces** the applicable smoothing owner and supplies the same effective position to camera, text and visibility. Do not stack it with Lenis or revive Cartesian camera interpolation. That is a measured fallback, not a second implementation to ship speculatively.

Explicit initial load, editor seek and route restoration may resolve directly to the requested pose. Ordinary fast scrolling must not be treated as a seek merely because progress changed by more than 0.08. Reverse travel follows the same curve backwards. A held final camera contains no independent orbit, dolly or in-place turn.

## 5. Perpetual motion with a small, explicit contract

Use **one shared 18-second phrase** as the first test preset. Each surface makes a small rigid lift and tilt around an authored pivot, with staggered phase offsets. Start at maximum excursions from rest of **8% of the subject height in lift and 10° in tilt**. These are test values, not rendered approval. The surfaces must visibly change orientation or relative position at 1/3/6-second comparisons while remaining calm over longer holds.

The object is already moving when revealed. The camera stops, but the object does not freeze or restart. Avoid 360-degree orbit, pursuit around a circle, sequential flashing or tiny point jitter as the only visible life. The complete silhouette must work at every phase.

### Lean source-to-runtime method

- Author the three base shapes and their pivots/axes in the saved Blender scene, working in a candidate copy. Use bounded rigid movement, not soft-body simulation, baked video or a second scene loader.
- Export an explicit **unanimated base pose**, plus a small versioned rig contract: group/object/pivot binding, site-space axes, period, phase offsets, angle/lift limits and permitted motion bounds. Temporarily bypass only the three preview rigid transforms; keep base placement and folds, then restore all source preview state in `finally`. A frame number alone is insufficient: staggered phases do not all become identity at the same time. Apply motion exactly once in the browser.
- Keep the packed 32-byte surfel record where practical. It already has an 8-bit `motionGroup`; current groups number 30. Add three explicitly identified rigid groups and a bounded transform table. Do not allocate 256 matrices or rebuild point buffers each frame.
- Use the existing RAF and renderer. Update three reusable transforms; both existing surfel passes consume the same transforms. Rotate normals with positions so facing, spacing and depth checks remain correct. Disable individual jitter and duplicate ambient transforms for these three rigid groups.
- Use a bounded periodic phase with compatible harmonics so position and velocity match at wrap. Pause autonomous advancement when hidden or reduced motion; resume without a phase jump. Reduced motion starts in a clear authored static pose. Changing the preference stops movement without withholding controls.
- Promote the exporter, runtime, validator and candidate assets together. Required motion data must fail closed on an unsupported adapter. Deliberately revise the scene capability/version and approved model set; do not weaken the old validators or revive the retired lens model. The binary stride and camera-track format need not change solely for this rig.

Compare Blender and browser poses at 0/25/50/75% of the cycle. Clearance diagnostics must use transformed positions and full motion bounds; a static rest-pose check cannot certify this moving object.

### Preserve the point budget honestly

Current profiles contain 30,000 mobile / 90,000 desktop points, with a 135,000-point master. A new model can cause proportional resampling to take points from every earlier landmark even if the total stays unchanged.

Freeze per-object allocations and recognition evidence for the earlier world. Give the finale an explicit bounded allocation. Prefer recovering only a terminal bank portion that is deliberately replaced; if that cannot preserve the method, record the additional point/byte cost and test it rather than silently thinning other models. No budget is accepted solely because the total count or build passes. Test the actual phone GPU cost before claiming 60 FPS.

## 8. Observable completion gates

1. **A real culmination:** unlabelled method/final stills have different subjects and silhouettes. The three final parts remain legible and complete in both viewports at every phase; the banks no longer dominate. No early full reveal behind method prose.
2. **No overlapping mess:** record the copy/action/focus envelope and the sculpture's projected swept envelope, including maximum point radius. They never intersect. Inspect both direction changes and all near/far handoffs for merged colour areas. No global thinning, title plate or text-shaped particle mask.
3. **Controlled camera:** sample the compiled map densely for monotone distance, continuous join derivatives and non-increasing terminal velocity. Check zero-length intervals and seek/restore behavior. Then record forward/reverse travel at 0.5 and 1.0 WU/s and an ordinary fast gesture. Review position, angular motion, visible speed and the actual final settle; screenshots alone cannot prove motion.
4. **Living final image:** capture phase extrema and 0/1/3/6/15/30/60-second holds, including at least three full 18-second cycles and the exact wrap boundary. Normal motion is visibly coherent, camera and UI stay fixed, and no part becomes edge-on or collides. Reduced motion remains complete and static at the scene layer.
5. **Usable invitation:** stop at 10/25/50% entry for two seconds; both actions complete without more input. Repeat after fast entry, reverse/re-entry, keyboard navigation, visibility changes, preference changes, scroll restoration and SPA return. Verify copy behavior without sending messages.
6. **Protected narrative and world:** exact approved facts and word counts; no blank retired title; five careers, six disciplines and fifteen clients retained. Every method thought has a natural readable focus stop. Earlier semantic poses/transforms and per-object recognition match the frozen baseline within a recorded export tolerance.
7. **Coverage:** Chromium and WebKit serial runs; 390×844 and 1440×1000 main profiles, supported 320px/short portrait, light/dark, representative high-contrast palettes, DPR1/2/3 and reduced motion. Preserve the intentional unsupported-short-landscape guard. Check resize/reflow at the held ending.
8. **Evidence and performance:** at least 75 scroll samples per main profile, fine samples at changed joins, native crops, motion recordings and a complete hold strip. Open every contact-sheet page and review it with the same three lenses. Compare a real-phone trace and stable GPU buffers; do not infer performance from contact sheets.
