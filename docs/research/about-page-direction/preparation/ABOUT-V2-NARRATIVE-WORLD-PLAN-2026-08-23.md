## Direction

The About world should feel like one material learning how to become something.
It begins as a small signal, expands into uncertainty, finds several ways through,
combines them into a landscape, survives challenge and change, becomes kinetic through
collaboration, then resolves into one coherent spatial form.

This is not a collection of objects illustrating sentences. It is an abstract argument
made from circles, surfaces, gaps, portals and motion.

The governing sequence is:

> seed → nebula → round portals → ribbon canyon → square loop → responsive lattice → lens

Each transition must reuse or visibly inherit the material from the previous stage. If
an element does not advance that sequence, it does not belong in the ride.

### The recurring idea: finding the gap

The sequence needs one idea more specific than “geometry coming together.” The proposed
motif is **finding the gap**. The camera repeatedly finds a way through complexity:

- the opening ring contains one small gap;
- the nebula opens a route through apparent disorder;
- round portals repeat different ways into the same problem;
- the ribbon canyon is the space created between interacting surfaces;
- square gates hold a stable opening while the camera's viewpoint changes;
- the responsive lattice coordinates itself to make a shared passage;
- the lens forms around one calm central clearing.

This gives the abstract world a consistent verb. The camera does not merely pass
scenery; it searches, enters, skims, turns, climbs and arrives.

## Confirmed design rules

1. The whole field uses the current time-of-day palette. Colour is structural, not a
   late recolouring effect.
2. Each individual portal is one colour. Successive portals cycle through the six
   discipline colours.
3. Preserve the existing pleasure of flying through round hoops and rolling through
   the square-gate loop.
4. Remove figurative props and isolated floating primitives. Surfaces and structures
   must form corridors, terrain, thresholds or destinations.
5. Camera proximity matters. The visitor should skim surfaces, enter gaps and pass
   through centres rather than observe every form from a polite distance.
6. Fog controls depth and arrival. It must not be used to disguise incomplete geometry
   or replace a designed transition.
7. The camera begins and ends level. Roll belongs to the square-gate chapter and has a
   narrative reason.
8. The final form must be earned from earlier material. A new unrelated object at the
   end is not a culmination.
9. Every major heading appears during a hold or a steady glide, never during peak roll,
   acceleration or a close surface pass.
10. A transition must be visible as a deformation. Crossfading, whitening or hiding one
    unrelated model before revealing another does not count as continuity.
11. Multicolour does not mean confetti. Colour belongs to a portal, ribbon, strand or
    current so that the palette remains readable at speed.

## Detailed stage direction

### 0. The quiet field

The first frame should be almost editorial. “About Me” and the description are the only
large information. Around them is a deep, low-density field of small circles with slow
independent drift. A small ring made from roughly 16–24 points sits beyond the title.
It is an aperture, not a decorative halo around the typography.

Those 16–24 points are high-priority anchors inside a much larger stable point pool.
The surrounding points already exist as the quiet field. They join the transformation
later, so the nebula does not appear to be generated from nowhere.

The opening must remain quiet long enough to read. Scroll first pulls the camera toward
the aperture; it does not immediately animate every point.

### 1. The signal opens

The camera passes through the ring as the question is completed. The ring's points
separate into an eroded, uneven cloud with dense islands, loose particles and open
pockets. This restores the strongest idea from the previous procedural sequence:
`cluster-v1` already created the compact spherical cloud, while `turbulent-field-v1`
created weighted chunks, organic warp and a geometric reading corridor.

The old implementation should be treated as behavioural evidence, not copied as a
second renderer. The new version should use the same fixed material identities so the
visitor sees the opening circle become the nebula rather than one scene fading into
another.

The transformation has two populations: the anchor ring breaks its perfect outline and
the surrounding field is pulled into several uneven currents. The protected gap begins
narrow, moves slightly off axis, then opens toward the first round portal. This should
feel like finding a route, not flying through a uniformly noisy volume.

### 2. Different ways in

The nebula should gradually develop circular gaps. The first full hoop is therefore a
consequence of the cloud, not a cut to a tunnel. Use at least twelve hoops so the six
discipline colours can repeat and establish rhythm.

Each hoop is one palette colour. Colour order follows the discipline list. When a
discipline label becomes active, the next corresponding hoop owns that colour; later
hoops repeat the cycle without repeating the labels. Labels remain readable editorial
content, not text attached to geometry in perspective.

### 3. Where disciplines meet: the ribbon canyon

The round portal corridor must not return to another floating-object field. Its circles
stretch and unspool into six broad dotted ribbons, one for each palette role. The ribbons
cross, fold and briefly support one another, creating the floor and walls of a long
canyon without becoming a literal landscape. The camera drops low, skims one ribbon,
passes between two intersecting sheets and climbs through an opening made by their
overlap.

The ribbons must not become six parallel lanes. Each changes the silhouette or route of
another. The result makes the “master of none” argument visible: the value comes from
disciplines affecting the same outcome, not sitting beside one another.

This is the longest reading chapter. Camera motion should be confident but calmer than
the portals. The client grid can appear as editorial evidence during a wider clearing;
it should not become landscape signage.

### 4. Direction under challenge

The ribbon canyon rises and narrows until its surfaces become rectangular thresholds.
This is the handoff to the square portals already working in Blender. Preserve the loop
and the authored roll, but connect their start and end to the surrounding material.

Each gate is one colour and the sequence alternates through the palette. The roll is a
visual analogue for challenging the same idea from different positions. The gates stay
structurally consistent while the camera orientation changes.

Translation remains attached to the camera path. Roll is one separate scalar channel
with approximately five purposeful keys: level on entry, two authored turns, the loop's
maximum rotation and a level exit. No per-frame or baked camera-transform keys should
be required for this chapter.

The collaboration title is still provisional. Its final language should express this
tension: an idea needs enough direction to remain coherent and enough openness to be
challenged. The visual chapter should not be locked to the narrower phrase “make the
work visible early.”

### 5. The work becomes shared: the responsive lattice

After the square loop, its straight edges lengthen into vertical strands. The existing
forest remains the visual reference, but the underlying structure is a responsive
lattice rather than a random collection of columns. The final gates visibly extrude
into the first strands, and those strands repeat into depth.

Every strand receives a stable palette role. Use all six roles throughout the depth,
with enough local variation that the scene reads as multicolour at every camera
position. Motion is grouped and causal:

- a slow vertical breathing wave keeps the whole field alive;
- travelling pulses move from strand to strand;
- neighbouring strands answer with delayed rises and falls;
- the camera corridor stays safe without becoming an obvious empty cylinder;
- reduced motion keeps the multicolour composition and removes the travelling pulses.

This chapter begins active and becomes increasingly coordinated. That progression maps
to making something, responding to it together, challenging it and carrying the central
idea through delivery.

### 6. The lens

The recommended culmination is **the lens**. Six coloured currents from the responsive
lattice bend inward to form two immense curved fields around a hollow, lens-shaped
volume. It is not a model of a camera lens. It is the spatial act of focusing: scattered
material now contributes to one shared clearing. The opening circle returns at
architectural scale.

The camera passes into the central void and comes to rest. The CTA sits in clear negative
space; the geometry surrounds it without forming a plate behind the text. Motion settles
into one restrained pulse so the ending feels resolved rather than frozen.

Two alternatives remain credible:

1. **Resolved surface** — all points settle into one immense folded surface. This is
   simpler and calmer, but less clearly echoes the opening aperture.
2. **Endless continuation** — the lattice opens into another portal and implies the ride
   continues. This preserves momentum, but weakens “Let’s begin” as a definite arrival.

The lens currently explains the complete trajectory best because it connects the first
question about attention to an ending about focus. A greybox should test whether it
feels like an earned destination or merely another portal.

## Concrete transition grammar

| Transition | Material inheritance | Camera gesture | Failure to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Seed → nebula | Ring anchors break formation while the existing field is drawn into irregular currents. | Slow pull becomes a short acceleration through the off-axis gap. | A tiny ring abruptly spawning thousands of unrelated points. |
| Nebula → round portals | Dense currents thin around circular voids; selected points settle onto the first hoop. | Camera discovers the first complete circle before entering the rhythmic corridor. | Cloud fades out, finished hoop fades in. |
| Round portals → ribbon canyon | Each hoop stretches longitudinally and opens into one coloured ribbon. | Camera drops below hoop centres and begins to skim the forming surfaces. | Returning to another field of floating props. |
| Ribbon canyon → square loop | Intersections straighten and fold until the ribbons frame rectangular openings. | Low flight becomes a climb; the first gate arrives while the camera is still level. | A hard cut from organic terrain to unrelated gates. |
| Square loop → responsive lattice | The final gate edges extrude into strands; repeated gates become depth rows. | Roll resolves to zero before the camera enters the moving field. | Carrying accidental tilt into the lattice. |
| Responsive lattice → lens | Strands curve inward in six coordinated currents and form the boundary of one empty volume. | Movement slows; the camera enters and stops inside rather than in front of the form. | Introducing a new finale object or ending with another fly-through. |

| 74–91% | Responsive lattice | Coordination | Medium glide through travelling waves |
| 91–100% | The lens | Resolution | Deceleration, level arrival and hold |

The desktop and mobile versions should use the same stage order and text bindings.
Mobile widens the safe corridor, softens peak roll and reduces lateral near-misses; it
does not receive a separate simplified narrative.

## Point material and colour contract

- Every point receives a stable palette role from zero to five. The current time-of-day
  scheme resolves those roles to actual colours without changing the composition.
- Palette roles remain attached to material through a transition. They are not replaced
  by white and then recoloured after the point reaches the clear zone.
- Fog reduces visibility toward the current scene background. It must not reveal distant
  points as opaque white bodies or act as a substitute for missing geometry.
- Multicolour fields use broad currents and clusters. Portals use one role per hoop,
  ribbons use one role per surface and lattice motion moves between role groups.
- Density is allocated by sampled surface area plus protected feature anchors, not by an
  equal point count per Blender object. Point spacing should feel consistent at the
  camera's designed viewing distance.
- Important silhouettes, narrow bands and portal rims receive a minimum anchor budget.
  Broad surfaces receive the remaining budget according to area. This prevents small
  complete shapes from disappearing and large meshes from becoming unexpectedly sparse.
- Only the current stage and its adjacent transition populations need full fidelity.
  Other stages can use their distance-appropriate page without changing point identity.

## A credible variation: one continuous topological ribbon

A stricter version could build the entire ride from one continuous six-colour surface.
The opening aperture would be a hole in that surface; the nebula would be a temporary
rupture; round and square portals would be differently shaped holes; the canyon would
be a fold; the lattice would be the surface splitting into strands; and the finale would
close those strands around the lens-shaped clearing.

This variant offers even stronger continuity and a simpler formal vocabulary. Its cost
is that it weakens the free particle field and irregular nebula that made the earlier
opening compelling. The recommended direction is therefore a hybrid: preserve free
particles for the seed and nebula, then establish the ribbon as the durable material
from the round portals onward. If the first greybox still feels like separate scenes,
the continuous-ribbon variant becomes the fallback.

## Copy implications

The current copy already provides the necessary argument. It does not need a wholesale
rewrite for the world to make sense.

- Keep the opening description and the question about attention.
- Keep “Modern problems refuse to stay in one category” as the round-portal threshold.
- Keep the disciplines as one dedicated beat inside the portal chapter.
- Split the long multidisciplinary passage into readable ribbon-canyon intervals; do not turn
  each paragraph into a new title.
- Continue refining the working-method title in the writing task. It should name the
  tension between direction and change, which connects the square loop to the responsive
  lattice.
- Keep “Let’s begin” concise. The spatial culmination should earn the invitation rather
  than asking the copy to explain it.

## Why the current approach drifted

The current surfel export is strong at sampling a Blender mesh accurately, retaining
uniform density, following a deterministic camera and revealing geometry through fog.
It is weak at expressing that one form becomes another. Independent models are sampled,
paged and revealed as separate objects. Placing more shapes beside the rail therefore
produces a decorated ride, even when the individual shapes look good.

The previous procedural system solved a different problem. It maintained a fixed point
pool and explicit correspondence from compact orb to turbulent field to later forms.
That continuity made the material feel purposeful, but the procedural world lacked the
Blender-authored camera and structural control now available.

The next architecture should combine those strengths:

1. Blender owns the camera rail, portal geometry, ribbon-canyon surfaces, lattice rest
   geometry and lens form.
2. Every Blender collection declares a narrative stage, text binding, palette mode,
   transition type and motion group.
3. The exporter samples each stage uniformly and computes deterministic correspondence
   between adjacent forms offline.
4. The runtime keeps one bounded point pool for the active transition and interpolates
   stable point identities. It does not rebuild buffers during scroll.
5. Fog remains a depth treatment. Stage transitions come from material motion and
   inherited geometry, not whitening or hiding unrelated objects.
6. Large static stages can still use camera pages, but adjacent stages share a protected
   transition population so no handoff pops or loses spatial chunks.

Without this stage-and-correspondence contract, increasing density will improve surface
resolution but will not solve the narrative problem.

## Proposed Blender organisation

Use one collection per stage:

- `ABOUT_STAGE_00_SEED`
- `ABOUT_STAGE_01_NEBULA`
- `ABOUT_STAGE_02_ROUND_PORTALS`
- `ABOUT_STAGE_03_RIBBON_CANYON`
- `ABOUT_STAGE_04_SQUARE_LOOP`
- `ABOUT_STAGE_05_RESPONSIVE_LATTICE`
- `ABOUT_STAGE_06_LENS`

Each exportable object should provide:

- `abs_stage_id`
- `abs_text_binding`
- `abs_palette_role`
- `abs_palette_mode` (`multicolour-field` or `single-colour-unit`)
- `abs_transition_mode`
- `abs_motion_group`
- `abs_camera_clearance_wu`
- `abs_feature_priority`

Timeline markers should identify the entrance, centre and exit of every stage. Roll keys
remain sparse and belong only to the square-loop interval.

## Recommended next experiment

Do not rebuild the complete ride next. Greybox only the stage volumes and camera beats:

1. opening aperture and nebula corridor;
2. round portal sequence;
3. ribbon-canyon silhouette and climb;
4. retained square loop;
5. responsive-lattice volume;
6. lens-shaped clearing.

Use neutral viewport geometry first, plus the actual camera and text timing. The test is
whether the silent ride still communicates opening, expansion, multiple ways in,
integration, challenge, collaboration and resolution.

If that reads clearly on desktop and mobile without colour or fog, proceed to the point
material and transition contract. If it does not, revise the stage order before spending
time on surfel density or finish.

The greybox passes only if:

1. each stage has a recognisable silhouette without colour, particles or fog;
2. every stage visibly produces the next one rather than disappearing before it;
3. the long reading passages occur during calm travel and all headings remain readable;
4. the round portals and square loop retain their existing sense of speed and proximity;
5. the camera enters and exits the square loop level with no accidental finale tilt;
6. the lens reads as an arrival and a transformation of the opening aperture, not as
   one more tunnel;
7. desktop and mobile communicate the same sequence without clipping the text or camera
   through geometry.
