R3 — FWA / CSS DESIGN AWARDS ABOUT-PAGE RESEARCH
Research date: 12 July 2026
Validated set: 13 unique CSSDA-recognized live sites with accessible About/Bio/Info/Studio content

BOTTOM LINE

The strongest About pages do not try to be miniature CVs. They make four decisions clearly:
1. What am I unusually good at now?
2. What selective path brought me here?
3. What do I believe that changes how I work?
4. What small human detail makes this voice memorable?

For Alexander, the clearest content model is Maria Vareva's compact trajectory + beliefs, paced with the image/artifact interruptions of Giacomo Agnello Modica and the confident levity of Bogdan Kolomiyets. Camila Rosa shows how to compress proof into expandable counts. AKIS.STUDIO supplies the alternating large-type / media rhythm, but its length and generic agency language should not be copied.

RECOMMENDED ONE-MINUTE SHAPE

- 0–10 sec: a present-tense positioning line that names the professional edge.
- 10–25 sec: three selective trajectory beats, each linked to a concrete change or client context.
- 25–38 sec: two working beliefs expressed as decisions, not values wallpaper.
- 38–50 sec: clients and proof in a compressed, optional layer.
- 50–60 sec: one personal/side-project reveal and a closing line with levity.

The core copy should target roughly 140–180 words. Lists, client detail, chronology, and side-work captions can expand on tap without adding to the first-pass reading time.

STRONGEST CASES

1. Maria Vareva — best compact narrative architecture.
   Current frontier -> selective five-step trajectory -> four beliefs -> six values in 151 words. Calm, credible, and readable in under a minute. Best source for Alexander's content skeleton.

2. Giacomo Agnello Modica — best integration of personality and side work.
   Clients and recognition sit beside drag theatre, an old bicycle, comfort food, divas, and self-produced merchandise. Interactive taste buttons reveal why the visual work looks the way it does. Best source for purposeful playful details.

3. Bogdan Kolomiyets — best confidence/likeability balance.
   A clear commercial promise, personal photos, proof metrics, and four plain-language beliefs end in a humorous call to action. Best source for confident copy that does not sound self-important.

4. Camila Rosa — best progressive disclosure.
   Two sentences explain the work; clients and achievements provide proof; awards/jury/exhibitions/etc. are compressed into tappable counts. Best source for keeping the primary read short.

5. AKIS.STUDIO — best scroll rhythm, weaker editing.
   Large kinetic headings alternate with photographs, short copy, team cards, and logo fields. The choreography is transferable; the 494-word, generic agency copy is not.

6. Jess Hill — best longform storytelling, clearest length anti-pattern.
   Turning points, mentors, stakes, archival images, intimate captions, cats, and illness create a vivid human arc. At 2,311 words it is an archive, not a one-minute About. Borrow its chapter logic and personal artifacts, not its volume.

REPEATED WINNING PATTERNS

- A present-tense role or frontier beats a chronological opening.
- Specific beliefs are stronger than adjectives: what gets removed, prototyped, challenged, or protected.
- Clients and awards work best as proof after the proposition, not as the hero.
- A visible number, date, or result carries more authority than 'passionate' or 'award-winning'.
- One personal artifact is more memorable than a paragraph of hobbies.
- Scroll chapters need alternating density: statement -> image/artifact -> proof -> statement.
- Optional interaction works when it reveals evidence or taste, not when it hides essential biography.
- Closing levity is most effective when it still advances the enquiry or human connection.

AVOID

- Exhaustive CV chronology in the primary path.
- Multi-role title stacks that read as keyword coverage.
- Generic passion/authenticity/excellence claims.
- Tool percentages and capability inventories.
- Repeated client, award, and testimonial proof.
- Motion on every word or element.
- Hover-only meaning on touch devices.
- A long client logo wall without grouping or hierarchy.
- Personality fragments that do not explain the work.
- A second interaction model that competes with vertical scrolling.

DESIGN IMPLICATIONS FOR ALEXANDER

- Keep vertical scroll as the only required navigation.
- Maintain a constant studio-window/background field so each section feels like a new arrangement in one room.
- Use the parked Spatial Scan point cloud as a narrative instrument, not ambient wallpaper: reveal a portrait/object/room only when the corresponding sentence warrants it.
- Let large type carry the transitions. One dominant sentence per viewport is enough.
- Insert two or three side artifacts (poster, experiment, small project) as draggable or expandable evidence tied to the adjacent idea.
- Allow client proof and deeper chronology to unfold on tap, while keeping the first-pass text visible and complete.
- End with an interaction that has emotional logic and levity, such as a small artifact that refuses to stay perfectly arranged, rather than an unrelated mini-game.
- Provide reduced-motion and no-WebGL equivalents; the narrative must remain coherent as plain text and static images.
