# About Me narrative questionnaire

## What this is for

This questionnaire supplied the raw material for the archived five-act structure in `../../archive/narrative-explorations/ABOUT-NARRATIVE-FRAMEWORK.md`:

**complexity → attention → structure → character → possibility**

It is not asking you to write polished website copy. Short answers, fragments, remembered scenes and imperfect wording are more useful than finished slogans. The eventual story will be written from your answers.

The 30 questions are arranged by their destination in the page:

| Act | Narrative job | What your answers need to supply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Threshold | Open a tension and promise a story. | A recognisable problem or judgement worth following. |
| 2. Personal lens | Show how your point of view formed. | A specific visual foundation, lived details and an early lesson. |
| 3. Expansion | Increase the scale and stakes. | The important turns from visual form into experiences and systems. |
| 4. Practice | Resolve the premise through present-day evidence. | What you do, how you work, who has trusted you and what makes it distinctive. |
| 5. Open ending | Turn the story toward the reader. | A credible ambition, human detail and clear invitation. |

## How to complete it

- Answer the 15 questions marked **CORE** first. They are enough to construct the first truthful narrative.
- Return to the remaining questions when you want to add texture, proof or personality.
- Write as you speak. Do not try to sound like a portfolio.
- Use one real example whenever possible: a project, decision, conversation, mistake or detail.
- If a fact needs checking, write **VERIFY** rather than guessing.
- If a question produces nothing useful, leave it blank. The final page does not need to contain everything.
- Preserve any phrase that feels naturally like you, even if it is rough. It may become a spatial line later.

## Act 2 — Personal lens: learning to see

**Reader question:** Why is Alexander the person telling this story?  
**Narrative job:** Ground the opening premise in a specific way of looking and learning.  
**Likely page treatment:** Large, left-aligned prose with a small group of optional early artefacts.

This section is about the foundation formed through composition, form and shape. University can provide context, but it is not the protagonist. Posters are one possible artefact among many.

### 7. What is your earliest vivid memory of noticing that visual form changes meaning? **[CORE]**

Name one or two decisions you began to see differently. A specific exercise, critique or failed attempt is especially useful.

### 9. Which lesson from your visual foundation still appears in your digital work today? **[CORE]**

### 11. Which early critique, mistake or unfinished piece changed your standards?

**Your answer:**


### 12. Does mentioning your education add something meaningful to this story?

## Act 3 — Expansion: from visual decisions to connected experiences

**Reader question:** How did that foundation become the work he does now?  
**Narrative job:** Show the important expansion in scale, connection and responsibility.  
**Likely page treatment:** Two or three short spatial beats, with a brief prose bridge only if a factual transition is essential.

This is not a complete career timeline. We need the smallest number of turns that make the current practice understandable.

### 13. What was the first project where you realised you were shaping behaviour, not only appearance? **[CORE]**

What could a person do, understand or decide because of the design? How did that feel different from arranging a surface?

### 14. Which two or three professional turns are essential to understanding your trajectory? **[CORE]**

### 15. When did the responsibility of the work become larger than the visible interface? **[CORE]**

## Act 4 — Practice: structure with character

**Reader question:** What does Alexander do, how does he work, and what makes the result distinctive?  
**Narrative job:** Resolve the central tension with present-day methods and credible evidence.  
**Likely page treatment:** Two or three left-aligned prose blocks with selective expandable proof.

This is the informational centre of the page. It needs to show both sides of the practice: giving complexity structure and keeping the outcome specific, responsive and alive.

### 19. Which one project best demonstrates your complete way of working? **[CORE]**

Please answer in four parts:

- What was unclear or difficult at the beginning?
- What did you personally contribute or decide?
- How did you work with other people?
- What changed in the work because of that contribution?

**Your answer:**


### 20. How would you describe your current practice without listing job titles? **[CORE]**

Complete this thought in your own language: “I help teams…” Then name two or three kinds of problem you are especially useful for.

**Your answer:**


### 21. How do structure and character enter the same process? **[CORE]**

Describe a real example where usability, coherence or a system mattered—and where motion, art direction, visual delight or an unusual decision made the result more valuable rather than merely more decorative.

**Your answer:**


### 22. What does your process look like when the starting point is genuinely unclear?

What do you examine first? What do you make early? What do you test? When do you know the work has found its organising idea?

**Your answer:**


### 23. What do international clients and collaborators consistently trust you to do?

Think beyond a formal title. What do people ask you to clarify, connect, challenge, visualise, prototype, refine or carry through?

**Your answer:**


### 24. Where does AI-assisted image-making genuinely improve your work?

Name one specific example. What did AI make possible, and which decisions, refinements or standards still depended on your judgement? If this is still experimental rather than a professional capability, say so.

## Act 5 — Open ending: possibility and invitation

**Reader question:** What does Alexander want to make next, and what should I do now?  
**Narrative job:** State a credible ambition, release the story and make one human invitation.  
**Likely page treatment:** A short spatial closing followed by a primary contact action and lighter secondary links.

The ending should not repeat the whole page. It should make the reader feel that the next project could continue the story.

### 25. What kind of problem would make you want to begin exploring immediately? **[CORE]**

Describe the tension, ambition or unknown that attracts you. Avoid answering only with a client name, sector or technology.

**Your answer:**


### 26. What do you want more of in the next stage of your practice? **[CORE]**

Name the kind of responsibility, collaboration, medium or creative territory you want to grow—not because it is fashionable, but because it continues something already present in your work.

**Your answer:**


### 27. Who should recognise themselves in the invitation? **[CORE]**

What kind of person or team do you want to hear from? What might they be trying to change, build or understand?

**Your answer:**


### 28. How would you invite that person to talk if you met them in real life?

Write two or three versions without polishing them. You may begin with “If you are trying to…” or use a phrase you would actually say.

**Your answer:**


### 29. What small personal detail should reward somebody who reaches the end?

Choose one piece of music, side experiment, object, image, habit or unfinished project. What does it reveal about you, and why does it belong after the professional story rather than inside it?

**Your answer:**


### 30. What should remain in the reader's mind after the page is closed?

Not a job title or list of capabilities. Describe the impression, standard or possibility you want them to remember.

## What happens after completion

The answers should be distilled in this order:

1. Extract exact phrases, concrete scenes and verified facts.
2. Fill the population worksheet in `../../archive/narrative-explorations/ABOUT-NARRATIVE-FRAMEWORK.md`.
3. Write one complete framework-led story before exploring tonal variations.
4. Check that the story works without animation.
5. Tighten it to approximately 200–240 core words.
6. Only then map the final text into spatial and editorial storyboard moments.
