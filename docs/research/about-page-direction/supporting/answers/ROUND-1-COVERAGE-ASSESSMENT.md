## What is already strong

Round 1 establishes a coherent point of view:

- the central tension is **clarity and character**, not complexity and simplicity as opposites;
- complexity should be layered and navigable rather than indiscriminately removed;
- simplification should amplify important things rather than erase them;
- generic systems and AI-generated best practice risk producing “copies of copies”;
- tactility, spatial memory, meaningful controls and a visible human footprint make digital work feel made for somebody;
- reliability, consistency and professionalism can coexist with delight and surprise;
- pragmatism matters under pressure, but character and integrity should survive;
- the American Heart Association work, the personal website and an iOS keyboard experiment offer early proof territories.

These answers give the threshold and central argument a much more natural vocabulary than the earlier abstract drafts.

## Information needed next

The second round should focus on six gaps without repeating the philosophy:

1. A selective but factually accurate career trajectory.
2. Formative experiences and the origin of Alexander's way of seeing.
3. Contribution-level evidence from two or three projects.
4. The actual working method: ambiguity, collaboration, prototyping, decisions and trade-offs.
5. A hierarchy of skills and a clear public positioning.
6. Future direction, ideal collaborators, personal texture and a credible invitation.

## Editorial caution

Several Round 1 answers refer to Apple, OpenAI, the Walkman and Tesler's Law. They are useful thinking references, but the public About story should ultimately rely more heavily on Alexander's own scenes, decisions and projects. Round 2 is designed to supply that evidence.
