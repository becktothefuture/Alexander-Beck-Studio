## What Round 2 establishes

- Language and technology are more useful origins than posters or a conventional design-school story.
- The career develops through a coherent expansion: visual language, digital interaction, behaviour and trust, then products, brands, systems and implementation.
- Alexander is most interested in ambiguous problems that require an organising idea rather than a predetermined visual treatment.
- His contribution combines creative direction, systematic thinking, immersion in the problem and enough technical fluency to carry an idea toward implementation.
- AI belongs in the practice as an amplifier of judgement and exploration, not a substitute for either.
- S&P Global, Yoti and the McCann website offer distinct proof of complexity, trust and restraint. AHA remains a strong recent example from Round 1.
- The desired working relationship is collaborative and educational: people should feel heard, understand the decisions and leave with work that remains useful.
- The future points toward unresolved questions involving AI, trust, privacy, robotics and other consequential uses of technology.

## Editorial implications

1. Keep most of the page in the present tense.
2. Use history only to explain why Alexander approaches the work this way now.
3. Let language and technology introduce the personal lens; keep the university as a possible footnote.
4. Use two named projects in the core story at most. Put further evidence into expandable proof.
5. Describe breadth through the contribution it enables, not through a long capability list.
6. Do not use the phrase “brings humanity to complex problems” as finished copy. Express the idea through behaviour, decisions and evidence.
7. Do not describe Alexander as someone who always “pursues the right solution.” Retain the intention while avoiding a claim of infallibility.
