THE DECISIVE READ

The best fit for Alexander is not a long agency manifesto. It is a 220–320 word personal story whose visual system does the emotional work. Righteous Creative proves that roughly 250 words can establish a promise, people, an origin and levity in one minute. Antoine Wodniack proves that a single dense trajectory sentence can coexist with highly expressive typography and spatial motion. Won J. You and Mark Woodland show how a belief can turn a varied career into a clear future-facing story. Fiddle and Better Off show how a side project, playlist, poster or experiment feels purposeful when it is evidence of a worldview rather than a miscellaneous gallery.

WHAT THE STRONGEST PAGES DO

1. Open with a useful belief, not a biography. “What changes because I am here?” is more compelling than job-title chronology.
2. Make confidence evidential. Dates, named clients, shipped tools, collaborators, teaching, quotes and outcomes keep bold statements from becoming vanity.
3. Change rhythm every 1–2 screens. Alternate large type, one short paragraph, a moving visual, a factual list, a personal fragment and a final light beat.
4. Keep scrolling primary. Hover, drag, audio and tap work best as optional expansions of a complete default story.
5. Give side work a reason. Bind posters, experiments or simulations to “current obsessions,” a time period, an influence, or a stated design belief.
6. Let one visual grammar connect the whole page. Lusion uses 3D connective tissue; Fiddle uses music; Antoine uses binary geometry. Alexander can use flat dots and the Spatial Scan point cloud.
7. End forward. The closing beat should combine aspiration with levity, not repeat a contact form.

RECOMMENDED ONE-MINUTE SHAPE FOR ALEXANDER

00–08s — One large belief or productive tension.
08–20s — One compressed trajectory sentence with selected client proof.
20–35s — Three specialties phrased as outcomes, each paired with a distinct dot-field response.
35–48s — Two or three side fragments (poster, experiment, small object) presented as evidence of curiosity.
48–56s — A future-facing sentence: what Alexander wants to make more of.
56–60s — A small playful ending that rewards a pointer move or tap without blocking the Contact route.

MOTION DIRECTION

Use native vertical scrolling and orchestrate one continuous point-cloud space behind it. The field can gather into typographic punctuation, widen into client constellations, briefly resolve into scan-derived fragments around side projects, and loosen at the final joke. Pointer or tap should perturb, unfold or temporarily label the field; it should never be required to reveal the next paragraph. Every scene must have a reduced-motion equivalent and visible text before animation completes.

HOLD ONTO

- One belief, one trajectory, three outcome-led specialties, selected client proof, two or three side fragments, one aspiration, one light ending.
- Expressive typography with short copy.
- Flat-dot/point-cloud continuity across scenes.
- Optional tap-to-expand micro-stories.
- Concrete evidence immediately after a confident claim.
- A visible native-scroll story that still works with reduced motion.

AVOID

- Chronological CV dumps, generic value words, giant client-logo walls and award-count climaxes.
- A second portfolio grid inside About.
- Unexplained hobby mosaics.
- Cursor tricks, drag or audio as mandatory navigation.
- Fixed virtual scrolling without semantic and reduced-motion fallbacks.
- More than roughly 300 core words if one-minute reading remains the constraint.
