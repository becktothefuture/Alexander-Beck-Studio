                      BEHIND THE BRIEF



                      Before we could design anything, we had to
The challenge




                      understand how the existing solution actually
                      worked. A mix of custom-coded Python scripts,
                      dense data tables, and internal-only Excel
                      visualisations. It wasn’t built for external users,
                      so our challenge was to translate that logic into
                      something intuitive and approachable without
                      losing its depth.

                      We spent time with internal teams, observing
                      how they worked with clients and piecing
                      together a system that had never been fully
                      documented. With few comparable tools on the
                      market, we weren’t just refining an interface. We
                      were defining what this kind of product could
                      and should be.

                   EARLY EXPLORATION

                   The early weeks of this project were all about
ideation




                   alignment. Not just on what we were building,
                   but how we wanted it to feel. We joined internal
                   working sessions, sketched messy flows, and
                   gradually pieced together a shared
                   understanding. These weren’t just product
                   choices. They were cultural shifts.

                   There was a lot of ambiguity to hold. But sitting
                   in the room, asking the right questions, and
                   proposing structure early helped us guide the
                   project forward.

                    Modular Thinking
ideation




                    One of my favourite challenges was giving
                    structure to something abstract. We needed to
                    show users what they were creating — and how
                    the parts fit together. We explored different
                    ways of visualising the logic, aiming for clarity
                    without oversimplification.


                    The modular building blocks — foundation,
                    schedule, method — became the
                    breakthrough. Visual, swappable, and
                    learnable. We even 3D printed them as a fun
                    keepsake, now sitting on my desk — a small,
                    tangible reminder of the thinking behind the
                    system.

                                              CHARM & RESTRAINT



                                              Making a dense interface feel light was a
ideation




                                              challenge I genuinely enjoyed. I focused on
                                              visual rhythm, micro-animations, clear
                                              typography and a strategic use of colour to
                                              empower users without distraction.

                                              My goal was to evolve the visual language of
                                              S&P Dow Jones into a design system with
                                              foresight. Modern, accessible, understated,
                                              with data always at the core. Even small choices
                                              like using simple text underlines for links were
                                              intentional. A nod to honesty in design, and a
                                              push to reimagine what clarity could look like
                                              across S&P Dow Jones’s product ecosystem.

                    TESTING WITH POWER USERS



                    One of the most valuable parts of the process
ideation




                    was getting the product in front of real users
                    early. We ran interviews and walkthroughs with
                    analysts, hedge fund managers, and index
                    engineers. People who knew the edge cases
                    better than anyone.

                    Their feedback didn’t just validate decisions. It
                    helped us simplify the right way. We learned
                    that clarity wasn’t about fewer options. It was
                    about smarter defaults, better groupings, and
                    showing the right control at the right time.
