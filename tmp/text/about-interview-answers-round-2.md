# ABOUT-NARRATIVE-ROUND-2-ANSWERS

Companion document preserving the substance and nuance of the spoken
interview before editorial rewriting.

## 1. Selective career chronology

Started in Computer Science before moving into Communication Design in
Mainz to study how visual language communicates ideas. Early agency work
focused on print and cultural projects. At Denaline became the digital
specialist, working with Lufthansa Group on interfaces, icon systems,
motion and SunExpress, learning systems thinking and developer
collaboration. Moved to London to join Yoti, where product, brand and
trust became inseparable and designed the anti-spoof hologram still used
today. Joined Hugo & Cat, later Hugo MRM/MRM, expanding into UX,
systems, front-end, motion, 3D, AI and organisational design. Alongside
agency work, built a freelance practice exploring AI and financial
education.

## 2. Why communication design?

Language has always fascinated me. As a polyglot I became interested in
how ideas are communicated. Communication Design felt like the meeting
point between language, technology and visual thinking.

## 3. When design became larger than composition

At Denaline and especially Yoti I realised design was about behaviour,
trust, systems and organisations rather than arranging visual elements.

## 4. Three defining projects

S&P Global Spice Index Builder: translating deep financial complexity
into a layered, elegant interface. McCann website: restraint and simple
interaction proving simplicity can be memorable. Yoti hologram:
combining physical movement and digital trust in a solution that has
endured.

## 5. Projects that taught difficult lessons

Bentley showed how ambition can exceed delivery timelines. S&P showed
that the final implementation matters because another agency completed
the build and the interaction nuance was lost.

## 6. Quiet habit

Systematic thinking and an ability to cut through complexity while
keeping ideas compelling.

## 7. Reputation

A creative thinker who brings humanity into complex problems, works
across media, uses AI to amplify thinking rather than replace it, and
creates bespoke solutions instead of formulaic ones.

## 8. Misleading labels

Not simply a UI designer or someone who makes things pretty. My work is
holistic and strategic, spanning product, brand, systems and
implementation.

## 9. Problems sought

Innovation, ambiguity, AI, trust, privacy, robotics and meaningful human
problems rather than mature products whose core challenges are already
solved.

## 10. Influences

Music's balance of experimentation and accessibility, Lego's physical
systems thinking and independent games' singular creative vision.

## 11. Team role

The creative voice that defines direction while inviting genuine
collaboration. I also naturally educate clients and teams around design
decisions.

## 12. Less of

Projects driven by internal politics or vanity rather than real user
value.

## 13. Misunderstanding

People underestimate how deeply I immerse myself in understanding a
problem. Good design requires much more than visual polish.

## 14. One lasting impression

Someone who can be trusted to pursue the right solution, even if it
requires inventing a new approach.

## 15. Desired feeling

Collaborators should feel heard, understood, cared for, and leave with
work that remains relevant over time.
