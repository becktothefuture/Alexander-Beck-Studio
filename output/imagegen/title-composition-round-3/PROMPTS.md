# Homepage title composition — reviewed prompt set

## Shared edit lock

Use the selected homepage mockup as the edit target. Change only the central title safe area. Preserve the exact 16:10 homepage composition, rounded white studio window, opaque black frame, bottom navigation, discipline legend, top-right paragraph, floating balls, Continuity control, footer, London clock, settings button, grain, palette, crop, and all surrounding UI.

Render exactly **“Alexander Beck”** and **“Designer & Technologist”**, with correct spelling and a clear ampersand. Add no other lettering. Keep every character readable. Use near-black lettering only.

## 01 — Embellished editorial calligraphy

Render both lines as one editorial calligraphic composition. Make “Alexander Beck” dominant with decisive high-contrast thick–thin strokes and compact, readable letterforms. Use exactly two large gestures: one open entry stroke before the A and one long exit stroke after Beck. Allow only one additional open loop to balance the silhouette; it must not enclose the name or become a crest.

Place “Designer & Technologist” on a stable baseline in the lower negative space at about one quarter of the name’s height, using a related but simpler calligraphic script. Keep flourishes behind or outside the descriptor and never through its letters. Aim for a contemporary cultural-publication masthead: assured, asymmetric, editorial, and sensuous through proportion rather than decoration.

Avoid hearts, wreaths, monograms, symmetrical emblems, bridal calligraphy, luxury-event logos, ornamental borders, filler curls, and excessive swashes.

## 02 — Thick creative-director hand

Render the text as a resolved two-level black brush-lettered composition. Draw “Alexander Beck” with a broad oblique chisel marker: firm vertical mass, sharp directional terminals, visible thick–thin contrast, and deliberate asymmetry. Keep counters open and every letter distinct. Use no more than two controlled joins and never overlap a stroke through a counter.

Set “Designer & Technologist” below at about one quarter of the name’s height in a narrower companion script written with the same tool at a lighter angle. Align it optically to the name’s width and keep it on one calm baseline. Keep the main strokes solid, with natural edge variation at only a few terminals. Aim for authored, forceful, adult authority.

Avoid rounded bubble forms, doodles, childish handwriting, underline scribbles, graffiti, rustic dry-brush texture, faux signatures, bouncy baselines, and cute irregularity.

## 03 — Thin architect’s script

Render the text as one restrained black technical-pen composition. Use a consistent fine monoline that remains visible at homepage and mobile scale, with a measured forward slant, open counters, and precise word spacing. Make “Alexander Beck” dominant and mostly connected through disciplined joins rather than formal copperplate loops.

Use one long horizontal gesture only, extending from a natural terminal to organise the composition. Set “Designer & Technologist” beneath the right half of the name at about one quarter of its height, using the same slant and line weight with slightly wider spacing. Aim for sparse, exact, cerebral, and quietly authoritative.

Avoid guide lines, grids, measurements, arrows, blueprint symbols, signature clichés, ornamental capitals, disappearing hairlines, repeated underlines, and notebook doodles.

## 04 — Mature pixel calligraphy

Render the text as one black pixel-calligraphic composition. Establish a graceful italic calligraphic skeleton, then build every stroke from hard-edged square cells on one consistent coarse pixel grid. Use stepped curves and exactly three stroke widths to preserve mature thick–thin rhythm. Keep pixels aligned with crisp edges and no antialiasing, blur, or scattered fragments.

Make “Alexander Beck” dominant. Place “Designer & Technologist” in the lower negative space at about one quarter of the name’s height, using the same cell size, a minimum two-cell stroke, and a coordinated italic pixel script. Use only two ligatures in the name and none in the descriptor. Aim for commissioned experimental type for a contemporary cultural institution: digital in construction and calligraphic in rhythm.

Avoid arcade fonts, blocky all-caps, monospaced terminal type, scanlines, CRT glow, chromatic effects, glitches, cursors, interface symbols, cyberpunk styling, and retro novelty.

## Independent review outcome

The reviewer ranked the initial directions: embellished editorial first, thin architect second, mature pixel third, and thick creative-director hand fourth. The final prompts were tightened to limit gestures, keep counters open, integrate the descriptor, protect exact text, preserve the homepage, and replace fashionable mood words with concrete construction rules.

The images were made with the built-in image-generation workflow. Direction 04 received one targeted correction so its descriptor uses the same pixel-calligraphic language as the name.
