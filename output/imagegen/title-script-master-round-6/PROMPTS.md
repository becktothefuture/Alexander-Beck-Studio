# Script title studies — direction sheet

Shared brief: replace only the central homepage title with the exact wording “Alexander Beck” and “Designer & Technologist.” Treat the words as one compact hand-lettered image. Keep the profession close to the name in scale and authority. Use one coherent tool, weight family, rhythm, and ink character. Preserve the existing homepage and ball composition.

Avoid technical or geometric lettering, simple marker writing, generic digital scripts, childish playfulness, bridal romance, giant initials, tiny descriptors, and decorative excess.

## 01 — Sign-painter italic

Disciplined broad-nib forms with compressed counters, crisp tapered joins, moderate thick–thin contrast, and restrained 1960s–70s editorial warmth.

- **01A:** four-line balanced stack with a near-square silhouette.
- **01B:** three-line offset stack with one controlled gesture connecting the name and profession.

## 02 — Seventies editorial brush

Rounder, softer brush construction with intelligent ligatures, generous bowls, pinched joins, and analogue print warmth without retro novelty.

- **02A:** four-line compact emblem with tucked descenders and tight leading.
- **02B:** three-line interlocking cascade with the ampersand acting as the central hinge.

## 03 — Contemporary chancery

Firm narrow broad-edge penmanship with supple italic lowercase, sharp humane joins, disciplined stroke modulation, and quiet European cultural-poster elegance.

- **03A:** four-line formal stack with equal visual darkness and carefully shared negative spaces.
- **03B:** three-line woven editorial lockup shaped like an art-book colophon.

## 04 — Interlocking script signet

Custom display lettering in which line endings, capitals, ascenders, and descenders are designed specifically to close the overall silhouette.

- **04A:** four-line woven rectangle anchored by the B and ampersand.
- **04B:** three-line sculpted oval with short inward gestures and restrained cross-line ligatures.
